\title{Persistent parametric manifold maps with calibrated support:
a reusable artefact for experiment guidance and data analytics}

\author{leanmap reference implementation}

\address{Reference implementation and reproduction record: the
\code{leanmap} package (\code{src/leanmap}), documentation under
\code{docs/}, and the frozen publication artefacts under
\code{publication/}.}

\begin{abstract}
Nonlinear dimension reduction is used in the physical and life sciences as a
lens: a dataset is embedded once, looked at, and the layout is discarded. That
workflow cannot support the two things an experimental or analytical pipeline
actually needs from a map --- a \emph{fixed frame} that successive batches enter
without redefinition, and an explicit statement of whether an incoming
measurement belongs to the region the frame describes at all. We present
\code{leanmap}, a parametric neighbour embedding designed around those two
requirements rather than around picture quality. A feed-forward encoder
$f_\theta:\R^D\to\R^d$, conditioned on soft assignments to a fixed landmark set
through feature-wise linear modulation, is trained against a multi-scale fuzzy
simplicial graph built under a user-supplied metric; the graph, the training
data, and the neighbour index are then discarded. The released artefact is a
single file holding encoder weights, landmarks, a per-landmark tangent-chart
support model, and conformal calibration scores, and is independent in size of
the training-set cardinality. Out-of-sample placement is one forward pass at
$3.4\,\mu$s per point against $781\,\mu$s for \code{UMAP.transform} on the same
data, and returns a distribution-free $p$-value for the hypothesis that the new
point is exchangeable with the training sample. We give the complete
mathematics of the construction: an $\varepsilon$-net with a size-corrected
duplicate radius, a Galerkin coarsening pyramid with a strictly monotone
membership squash, a composite objective combining fuzzy cross-entropy with
Gram-domain local rigidity, a classical-MDS geodesic gauge and a scale-free
density-ordering term, and category-conditional (Mondrian) conformal
calibration with a closed-form minimum-norm repair onto the acceptance region.
We treat map \emph{persistence} --- whether refitting reproduces the same map
--- as a first-class reported quantity rather than an assumption, and show that
it separates sharply by data type: manifold datasets return the same map to
rank correlation $0.998$ across seeds, while a clustered dataset shares fewer
than two neighbours in five. We close with an artefact contract and a
diagnostic table intended to make published maps auditable and reusable.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
%==============================================================================

\subsection{Maps as instruments, not illustrations}

Neighbour embeddings --- $t$-SNE~\cite{vandermaaten2008tsne},
UMAP~\cite{mcinnes2018umap}, and their descendants --- have become a default
step in scientific data analysis. In their usual form they solve a
\emph{transductive} problem: given $N$ points, produce $N$ two-dimensional
coordinates by treating those coordinates as free optimisation variables. The
output is a layout. It is an excellent illustration and a poor instrument,
because it has no defined behaviour on any point that was not in the original
fit.

The distinction matters as soon as a map is used for anything other than
looking. Three settings make this concrete.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Experiment steering.} A beamline, microscope, or synthesis robot
  produces measurements in a stream. If a map is to route decisions --- collect
  more here, stop there, flag this for a human --- successive batches must land
  in \emph{the same} coordinate frame as the batches that established the
  decision boundaries. Refitting between batches redefines the frame and
  silently invalidates every stored threshold.
\item \textbf{Analytics on a shared reference.} A group that has explored a
  corpus once wants the resulting chart to become a reusable coordinate system:
  regions get named, thresholds get set, downstream models consume the
  coordinates. This requires that the map be a saved function, that its saved
  form not grow with the corpus, and that calling it twice on the same input
  give the same answer.
\item \textbf{Triage of the unfamiliar.} Any map defined only by interpolation
  among training neighbours will place an arbitrary input \emph{somewhere},
  usually somewhere plausible. A frame used for decisions has to be able to say
  ``this measurement is not in the region I describe'' --- and to say it with a
  calibrated error rate rather than a heuristic.
\end{itemize}

None of these are served by a layout. All three are served by a \emph{model}:
a function that can be saved, called, versioned, and audited, accompanied by an
explicit description of its domain of validity.

\subsection{What this paper contributes}

\code{leanmap} (\emph{LEarn ANother MAPping}) is a parametric neighbour
embedding built to that specification. Its structure is conventional in one
respect --- the geometric term is UMAP's fuzzy cross-entropy against a fuzzy
simplicial graph --- and unconventional in the rest. The contributions are:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{An artefact contract} (\S\ref{sec:contract}) stating precisely
  what a released map must carry, what must be recorded alongside it, and under
  which conditions its coordinates rather than merely its neighbour rankings
  may be relied on.
\item \textbf{A landmark-conditioned parametric encoder} (\S\ref{sec:model}).
  Soft assignments to a fixed landmark set drive feature-wise linear
  modulation~\cite{perez2018film} of an MLP backbone. The landmarks serve three
  simultaneous roles --- conditioning code, inverted-file index for graph
  construction, and support model for out-of-distribution scoring --- so the
  same $L\times D$ matrix that makes the encoder expressive also bounds the
  cost of inference and defines the domain of validity.
\item \textbf{A duplicate-robust, multi-scale graph} (\S\ref{sec:graph}).
  Near-duplicates are collapsed by an $\varepsilon$-net whose radius is
  estimated from the full nearest-neighbour distribution with an explicit
  correction for sample size, then merged across Voronoi boundaries by a
  union--find halo pass. Coarse levels are produced by Galerkin edge
  contraction, which inherits connectivity from the fine graph and therefore
  cannot invent short-circuits across a fold.
\item \textbf{A composite objective with separable, scale-free
  regularisers} (\S\ref{sec:loss}): Gram-domain as-rigid-as-possible local
  rigidity with an optional tangent projection, a classical-MDS geodesic gauge
  with a reported non-embeddability diagnostic, and a density term that
  constrains the \emph{ordering} of neighbourhood radii but deliberately not
  their magnitude.
\item \textbf{A calibrated support model} (\S\ref{sec:conformal}) with three
  layers: per-landmark tangent charts as the nonconformity score,
  distribution-free conformal $p$-values, and category-conditional (Mondrian)
  calibration that distinguishes families of anomaly rather than merely
  flagging them. A closed-form minimum-norm repair returns the cheapest
  modification of an input that would pass the test.
\item \textbf{Persistence as a reported metric} (\S\ref{sec:persistence}). We
  argue that the property a saved map actually sells is reproducibility under
  refit, that no standard embedding metric measures it, and that it must be
  reported separately for coordinates and for neighbour rankings because the
  two fail differently.
\item \textbf{A practitioner's diagnostic table} (\S\ref{sec:guidance}) linking
  each internal quantity the implementation already emits to the failure it
  detects and the action it implies.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{What is deliberately not claimed}

The measurements in \S\ref{sec:results} are reported with seed spread, and a
difference is called \emph{resolved} only when it exceeds twice the pooled
seed standard deviation. Under that rule several claims one might expect are
not supported. \code{leanmap} does not beat UMAP on label-transfer accuracy or
trustworthiness on clustered data. The FiLM conditioning apparatus is resolved
against its own concatenation control on exactly one axis of one dataset. The
inference speed-up is real but is a consequence of replacing a variable-size
search with a fixed-size one, not of eliminating search. And the coordinates of
a map fitted to clustered data are, on our measurements, not stable enough to
be treated as an artefact at all --- only its neighbour rankings are. We state
these plainly because a paper about reusable artefacts that overstates their
reliability defeats its own purpose.

\section{The artefact contract}
\label{sec:contract}
%==============================================================================

Before any mathematics it is worth writing down what is being built, because
the design of every component below follows from it.

\begin{definition}[Map artefact]
\label{def:artefact}
A \emph{map artefact} is a tuple
\[
\mathcal{A}
=
\bigl(f_\theta,\;M,\;d_X,\;s_{\mathrm{nat}},\;\mathcal{S},\;
\{s_j\}_{j=1}^{n_{\mathrm{cal}}},\;\mathcal{D}\bigr)
\]
consisting of an encoder $f_\theta:\R^D\to\R^d$; landmark coordinates
$M\in\R^{L\times D}$; the ambient metric $d_X$ and its fitted natural scale
$s_{\mathrm{nat}}$; a support model $\mathcal{S}$ inducing a nonconformity
score $s(\cdot)$; a sorted vector of calibration scores; and a diagnostic
record $\mathcal{D}$.
\end{definition}

The artefact must satisfy six requirements, each of which drives a specific
design decision later in the paper.

\begin{description}[leftmargin=0pt,style=nextline]
\item[\textnormal{(R1)} Closure.] Evaluating $\mathcal{A}$ on a new point
  requires nothing but $\mathcal{A}$. No training data, no neighbour index, no
  graph. \emph{Consequence:} the map must be parametric and the support model
  must be summarised by the landmarks (\S\ref{sec:model},
  \S\ref{sec:conformal}).
\item[\textnormal{(R2)} Bounded size and cost.] $\abs{\mathcal{A}}$ and the
  cost of one evaluation are independent of $N$. \emph{Consequence:}
  out-of-sample placement is $L$ distance computations plus one forward pass,
  a fixed budget chosen at fit time (\S\ref{sec:inference}).
\item[\textnormal{(R3)} Metric agnosticism.] The ambient geometry is supplied by
  the user, not assumed Euclidean. \emph{Consequence:} a capability-bearing
  metric layer that records whether a metric admits an $\ell_2$ transform, is a
  true metric, and is differentiable, and degrades gracefully when it is not
  (\S\ref{sec:metric}).
\item[\textnormal{(R4)} Declared domain.] The artefact states where it is valid
  and with what error rate. \emph{Consequence:} a support model with
  finite-sample distribution-free guarantees under exchangeability
  (\S\ref{sec:conformal}).
\item[\textnormal{(R5)} Differentiability.] $f_\theta$ composes into downstream
  networks and can be inverted or navigated by gradient methods.
  \emph{Consequence:} every loss term differentiates the objective directly
  rather than using a clipped-force surrogate (\S\ref{sec:loss}).
\item[\textnormal{(R6)} Auditability.] Everything needed to decide whether to
  trust the map is recorded, including the evidence that it might not be
  trustworthy. \emph{Consequence:} the diagnostic record $\mathcal{D}$ of
  \S\ref{sec:guidance}, and the persistence protocol of
  \S\ref{sec:persistence}.
\end{description}

\begin{remark}[Why (R6) is not decoration]
Requirements R1--R5 are properties of the implementation and can be checked
once. R6 is a property of a \emph{particular fit to particular data} and cannot.
An artefact that satisfies R1--R5 perfectly and whose classical-MDS gauge target
is not embeddable, or whose triplet retention sits at chance, or whose refit
returns a different layout, is a well-engineered instrument reporting nothing.
Every quantity in \S\ref{sec:guidance} exists because there is a way for a fit
to fail silently in exactly that respect.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Capability-bearing metrics}

A map that is only correct under $\ell_2$ is not usable for scientific data,
where the meaningful comparison is frequently a correlation distance between
spectra, an $\ell_1$ between normalised distributions, a Frobenius norm between
matrix-valued features, or a bespoke callable. The implementation therefore
carries a metric as a record rather than a function:

\begin{definition}[Metric specification]
\label{def:metricspec}
A \emph{metric specification} is
$\mathcal{M}=(\mathrm{name},\,\phi,\,T,\,\epsilon_{\mathrm{ex}},\,
\mu,\,\delta,\,s_{\mathrm{nat}})$ where $\phi:\R^{n\times D}\times\R^{m\times
D}\to\R^{n\times m}$ is the exact batched distance; $T$ is an optional map with
$\norm{T(x)-T(y)}_2$ monotone in $\phi(x,y)$; $\epsilon_{\mathrm{ex}}$ records
whether $T$ preserves $k$-NN sets exactly; $\mu$ records the triangle
inequality; $\delta$ records differentiability in the second argument; and
$s_{\mathrm{nat}}$ is a fitted scale. The metric used everywhere downstream is
$d_X(x,y)=\phi(x,y)/s_{\mathrm{nat}}$.
\end{definition}

The flags are load-bearing, not documentation. $T$ and $\epsilon_{\mathrm{ex}}$
decide whether an approximate-nearest-neighbour index may be used for candidate
generation, with exact re-ranking under $\phi$ regardless. $\delta$ decides
whether landmark coordinates are learnable: if $\phi$ is not differentiable in
its second argument the landmarks are frozen after initialisation and a warning
is emitted, rather than silently receiving zero or garbage gradients. $\mu$
governs whether the geodesic constructions of \S\ref{sec:geo} are meaningful.

\begin{definition}[Natural scale]
\label{def:natscale}
On a subsample of $\min(10^4,N)$ points, let $r_k(i)$ be the $k$-th smallest
distance from $x_i$ under $\phi$. Then
$s_{\mathrm{nat}}=\med_i r_k(i)$, with $k=\code{n\_neighbors}$.
\end{definition}

Dividing by $s_{\mathrm{nat}}$ makes every neighbourhood radius, every
$\varepsilon$, and every cover score an $\mathcal{O}(1)$ quantity independent
of the physical units of the input. This is what allows a single set of
defaults to transfer between, say, unit-cell parameters in \AA{} and normalised
pair-distance distributions.

\subsection{Composite view metrics}
\label{sec:viewmetric}

Scientific features often factor into parts that should be compared
differently: a shape and a scale, a composition and a temperature, a spectrum
and an acquisition condition. The implementation supports this through
\emph{views}. A view is a differentiable map $v_f:\R^D\to\R^{D_f}$; each view
carries its own metric $\phi_f$, its own anchor set, and optionally a weight
$w_f$ contributing to a composite scoring metric,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:viewmetric}
d_X(x,y)^2
=
\sum_{f:\,w_f\neq\varnothing}
w_f\left(\frac{\phi_f\bigl(v_f(x),v_f(y)\bigr)}{c_f}\right)^{\!2},
\qquad
c_f=\med_i\, r_1^{(f)}(i),
\end{equation}
with $c_f$ the median $1$-NN distance within block $f$, fitted on a subsample
so that blocks with incomparable units contribute comparably. A canonical use
is the scale--shape quotient: $v_{\mathrm{shape}}(x)=x/\norm{x}_1$ carrying the
primary conditioning role, and $v_{\mathrm{scale}}(x)=\log\norm{x}_1$ entered
as a monotone axis constraint (\S\ref{sec:roles}), so that magnitude becomes an
interpretable coordinate rather than a nuisance direction folded into the
layout.

\subsection{Intrinsic dimension}
\label{sec:id}

Several constructions need an estimate of intrinsic dimension. We use the
Levina--Bickel maximum-likelihood estimator~\cite{levina2005} on a subsample of
$\min(2000,N)$ points with $k=10$:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lb}
\hat m
=
\left[
\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i}\frac{1}{k-1}\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}
\log\frac{r_k(i)}{r_j(i)}
\right]^{-1},
\end{equation}
clamped to $[1,D]$, with non-finite and non-positive per-point terms discarded
before averaging. It appears in the sample-size correction of
\S\ref{sec:epsilon} and is reported alongside the density budget of
\S\ref{sec:density}; it is never used to set a loss weight.

%==============================================================================
\section{Graph construction}
\label{sec:graph}
%==============================================================================

Training targets a fuzzy simplicial set built once, before any optimisation,
and discarded afterwards. The construction has five stages: duplicate collapse,
landmark selection, neighbour search, membership smoothing, and multi-scale
coarsening.

\subsection{Duplicate collapse and the $\varepsilon$-net}
\label{sec:epsilon}

Scientific corpora are rarely i.i.d.\ samples from a manifold. Structural
databases contain re-depositions of the same molecule; instrument streams
contain repeated measurements of a stable state; simulation outputs contain
near-identical frames. Such mass distorts a $k$-NN graph badly: a cluster of
$q$ near-identical rows consumes $q$ neighbour slots from every point near it,
and contributes $\mathcal{O}(q^2)$ edges of essentially unit membership that
dominate the sampler.

\begin{definition}[Duplicate radius]
\label{def:eps}
Let $r_1(i)=\min_{j\neq i}\phi(x_i,x_j)$ be the exact $1$-NN distance. Then
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:eps}
\varepsilon
=
\begin{cases}
Q_{0.01}\bigl(\{r_1(i)\}_{i=1}^{N}\bigr),
  & \text{full pass affordable},\\[4pt]
Q_{0.01}\bigl(\{r_1(i)\}_{i\in S}\bigr)\cdot
  \left(\dfrac{\abs{S}}{N}\right)^{1/\hat m},
  & \text{otherwise, } \abs{S}=\min(10^4,N),
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
with $\hat m$ from~\eqref{eq:lb}. If the resulting value is $\le 0$ --- which
happens when exact duplicates occupy more than the bottom percentile --- it is
replaced by the median of the strictly positive $r_1(i)$, or by $10^{-12}$ if
none are positive.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Why the correction is necessary]
\label{rem:epsdrift}
On an $m$-dimensional manifold sampled at density $n$, the typical $1$-NN
distance scales as $n^{-1/m}$. Reading $\varepsilon$ off a fixed-size subsample
therefore makes it a function of \emph{dataset size} rather than of the data:
estimating on $10^4$ of $10^6$ points inflates $\varepsilon$ by
$100^{1/m}\approx 2.2$ at $m=6$, precisely in the regime where deduplication is
doing real work. Correcting by $(\abs{S}/N)^{1/\hat m}$ removes the leading
size dependence. The exact path is taken for $N\le 2\times 10^4$; above that, an
inverted-file index supplies eight candidate neighbours per row and the
distance is still evaluated exactly under $\phi$, so the value is exact whenever
the candidate list contains the true nearest neighbour. Which path was taken,
the fraction of exact zeros, and the deciles of $r_1$ are all recorded in
$\mathcal{D}$.
\end{remark}

The resolved $\varepsilon$ is written back into the saved configuration. A
refit therefore reuses the stored radius rather than re-estimating one, which
makes the merge scale \emph{a fixed property of the artefact} reached through a
data-driven estimator --- a direct consequence of R6, and the difference between
``explored once and saved'' and ``re-derived each time with slightly different
results''.

\paragraph{Greedy net.}
Within each top-$1$ landmark bucket, points are visited in random order; $x_i$
joins an existing cell with representative $x_{r_j}$ if
$\min_j \phi(x_i,x_{r_j})\le\varepsilon$, and otherwise opens a new cell.
Cell multiplicity $w_i$ is the number of raw members. The compression ratio
$N/R$ is recorded; a value near $1$ means the net was a no-op and $\beta$
(below) is inert.

\paragraph{Halo pass across bucket boundaries.}
Growing the net inside buckets leaves a systematic gap: a near-duplicate pair
separated by a Voronoi boundary is never compared, so both survive as distinct
cells. These are not incidental survivors. The bucket boundary is exactly where
the conditioning code $a(x)$ switches, so a duplicate straddling it is the one
most likely to receive two different modulations and hence two different
embedding positions.

A single post-pass closes them. Candidate pairs are drawn from the top-$c$
landmark shortlist already computed for the inverted index, restricted to pairs
in \emph{different} top-$1$ buckets, and accepted when $\phi\le\varepsilon$. All
candidates are evaluated against the \emph{pre-merge} representative set, and
accepted pairs are collapsed by union--find. The outcome is therefore the
connected-component partition of the accepted-pair graph, which does not depend
on visitation order: a chain $A\!-\!B\!-\!C$ yields one cell rather than
whichever pair happened to be seen first. Component roots are the lowest
representative index, so the labelling is reproducible across seeds; cell
weights add.

One invariant is deliberately surrendered: a merged cell no longer lies inside
a single landmark bucket. Every consumer of the bucket-to-cell map --- the
inverted-index shortlist, cell-to-member expansion in the edge sampler, and
ordinal mid-point sampling --- either recomputes bucket assignments from the
surviving representatives or indexes raw points, so none depends on the
discarded invariant.

\subsection{Landmarks}
\label{sec:landmarks}

The landmark set $\{M_\ell\}_{\ell=1}^L$ is the single most reused object in
the artefact: it is the conditioning code (\S\ref{sec:affinity}), the inverted
index for neighbour search (\S\ref{sec:knn}), the node set of the geodesic gauge
(\S\ref{sec:geo}), and the support model for out-of-distribution scoring
(\S\ref{sec:conformal}). Three selection schemes are provided.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Ambient farthest-point sampling.} From a random seed index,
  iterate $j\leftarrow\argmax_i\min_{\ell\in C}\phi(x_i,M_\ell)$. Cheap,
  deterministic given the seed, and over-samples boundaries and extreme tips
  --- which is desirable for a cover and undesirable for a conditioning code.
\item \textbf{Geodesic farthest-point sampling.} The same greedy max--min rule
  on shortest-path distances in the symmetrised $k$-NN distance graph
  $G\leftarrow\max(G,G^\top)$, computed by Dijkstra. Unreachable nodes are
  treated as farther than any finite path, so every connected component is
  covered. This matters on manifolds that fold back on themselves, where two
  ambient-nearby points can be far apart along the sheet.
\item \textbf{Geodesic Poisson-disk (blue-noise) sampling.} Estimate a packing
  radius $r$ as the coverage radius of a short $L$-point geodesic
  farthest-point pass, then dart-throw: visit points in random order and accept
  $x_i$ iff its geodesic distance to every accepted point is at least $r$,
  running one Dijkstra per acceptance. The result is a maximal $r$-separated
  (Delone) set with interior-uniform coverage, which farthest-point sampling
  does not provide.
\end{enumerate}

Each point is then assigned to its $c=8$ nearest landmarks, giving both a
top-$1$ bucket (used by the $\varepsilon$-net and the ordinal sampler) and a
top-$c$ shortlist (used by the inverted index and the halo pass).

\subsection{Neighbour search}
\label{sec:knn}

On the $R$ representatives a $k$-NN graph is built in one of three modes.
\emph{Brute} computes the full $R\times R$ distance in chunks. \emph{IVF} takes
candidates as the union of the buckets of the query's top-$c_{\mathrm{search}}$
landmarks, and re-ranks exactly. \emph{ANN} uses a FAISS IVF-Flat or HNSW index
built on $T(X)$ with over-fetch, again re-ranked exactly under $\phi$; this
path is available only when the metric declares an $\ell_2$ transform. In every
mode the returned distances are exact; only the candidate set is approximate.
Recall against a brute-force probe set is measured and the search budget
increased if it falls below $0.9$. Both the mode and the measured recall enter
$\mathcal{D}$.

\subsection{Smooth memberships}
\label{sec:smoothknn}

We follow UMAP's fuzzy simplicial set
construction~\cite{mcinnes2018umap,spivak2012fuzzy}.

\begin{definition}[Local connectivity and bandwidth]
\label{def:smoothknn}
For representative $i$, let $\rho_i$ be the $\code{local\_connectivity}$-th
smallest \emph{strictly positive} $k$-NN distance. Solve $\sigma_i>0$ by
bisection so that
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sigma}
\sum_{j\in\Nb_k(i)}
\exp\!\left(-\frac{\max(d_{ij}-\rho_i,\,0)}{\sigma_i}\right)
=
\log_2 k ,
\end{equation}
with the upper bracket doubled until the constraint is satisfiable, at most
$64$ bisection steps, and tolerance $10^{-5}$. Enforce the floor
$\sigma_i\ge\code{min\_sigma\_frac}\cdot\overline{\rho}$ with
$\code{min\_sigma\_frac}=10^{-3}$.
\end{definition}

Directed memberships are
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:pij}
p_{ij}
=
\exp\!\left(-\frac{\max(d_{ij}-\rho_i,\,0)}{\sigma_i}\right),
\qquad j\in\Nb_k(i),
\end{equation}
so $p_{ij}=1$ for the $\code{local\_connectivity}$ nearest neighbours by
construction, guaranteeing that no point is isolated. The number of rows
failing to bracket, hitting the floor, or being degenerate (all $k$ distances
within a relative margin of $\rho_i$) is counted; a degenerate fraction above
$0.05$ triggers a warning, since it indicates duplicate-dominated data for
which $\varepsilon$ or $\code{local\_connectivity}$ should be raised.

\subsection{Symmetrisation, multiplicity, and backbone}
\label{sec:fuzzyunion}

Let $P$ be the sparse directed matrix of memberships. Symmetrisation uses the
probabilistic $t$-conorm (fuzzy union),
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:fuzzyunion}
P^{\mathrm{sym}} = P + P^\top - P\circ P^\top,
\end{equation}
\emph{not} mutual $k$-NN. The distinction is consequential for sparse regions:
mutual $k$-NN discards one-sided memberships and therefore orphans exactly the
points a scientific map most needs to place --- rare states, boundary
conditions, outlying compositions.

Cell multiplicity re-enters as
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:multiplicity}
P^{\mathrm{sym}}_{ij}\;\leftarrow\;
P^{\mathrm{sym}}_{ij}\,(w_i w_j)^{\beta},
\qquad
\beta=\code{beta\_multiplicity},
\end{equation}
followed by normalisation $\max_{ij}P^{\mathrm{sym}}_{ij}=1$.

\begin{remark}[$\beta$ states what a repeated row means]
\label{rem:beta}
The exponent $\beta$ encodes a modelling claim about the corpus, not a solver
setting, which is why it is exposed rather than fixed. At $\beta\to 0$
multiplicity is ignored and an $\varepsilon$-cell contributes as a single
point: the right choice when duplicates are a deposition or acquisition
artefact carrying no density information --- repeated database submissions of
one structure, redundant crystal forms of one compound, a detector parked on a
stable state. At $\beta\to 1$ the reweighting is proportional and multiplicity
is treated as genuine sampling density the layout should reproduce. The default
$\beta=0.5$ lets a cell of weight $w$ act like $\sqrt{w}$ points. Note the
coupling to $\varepsilon$: together they decide how much density a
duplicate-heavy region is credited with, so a change to one should be read
against the other.
\end{remark}

An optional landmark backbone inserts edges of fixed weight
$\lambda_{\mathrm{bb}}=0.01$ along a minimum spanning tree of the
landmark--landmark distance matrix, mapped to nearest representatives, merged
into $P^{\mathrm{sym}}$ by elementwise maximum and renormalised. The training
edge list retains the strict upper triangle with positive weight.

\subsection{The Galerkin pyramid}
\label{sec:pyramid}

A single-scale fuzzy graph carries only local attraction: distant regions of the
manifold exert no force on one another and are free to drift apart, which
produces layouts that are locally faithful and globally arbitrary. Coarse
levels supply the missing long-range term.

\begin{definition}[Level sizes]
With finest representative count $R_0$ and ratio $\varrho=4$,
\[
R_\ell^{\mathrm{target}}
=
\max\!\left(\mathrm{round}\!\left(R_0/\varrho^{\ell}\right),\;
\code{pyramid\_min\_reps}\right),
\quad \ell=1,\ldots,\code{pyramid\_scales},
\]
and coarsening stops when the target is no smaller than the previous level. The
number of levels actually built is therefore often smaller than
$\code{pyramid\_scales}+1$; with the defaults, four levels require
$N\gtrsim 1.7\times 10^4$.
\end{definition}

Coarsening from level $\ell$ to $\ell{+}1$ proceeds as follows. Coarse
representatives are chosen by farthest-point sampling among the current
representatives; every fine representative is assigned to its nearest coarse
representative by a map $\pi$, and the compressed-row cell maps compose down to
raw points. Edge weights are then aggregated by \emph{Galerkin contraction}:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:galerkin}
W^{(\ell+1)}_{AB}
=
\sum_{\substack{(i,j)\in E_\ell \\ \pi(i)=A,\ \pi(j)=B,\ A\neq B}}
w^{(\ell)}_{ij},
\end{equation}
with intra-cell self-loops discarded. A coarse edge exists only where fine
edges already cross, so connectivity is \emph{inherited}: unlike an Isomap-style
coarse graph built from coarse-node distances, this construction cannot
manufacture a shortcut across a fold, and thin bridges are strengthened rather
than invented.

\paragraph{Membership squash.}
Aggregated weights are heavy-tailed --- one wide bridge can exceed a thin one
by orders of magnitude --- and must be mapped into $(0,1)$ to be a membership.
Three maps are available:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:squash}
\tilde w
=
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{w}{w+q_{0.99}(w)}, & \code{rational\_q99}\quad\text{(default)},\\[10pt]
\dfrac{w}{w+q_{0.50}(w)}, & \code{rational},\\[10pt]
\min\!\bigl(w/q_{0.99}(w),\,1\bigr), & \code{quantile\_clamp}.
\end{cases}
\end{equation}

\begin{remark}[The anchor point, not the shape, is what matters]
\label{rem:squash}
The clamp is not monotone: everything above the $0.99$ quantile is flattened to
a common weight of exactly $1$ --- precisely the strongest long-range bridges,
which are the edges a coarse-heavy schedule exists to exploit. Replacing it
with a strictly monotone rational map is the obvious fix and, anchored at the
median, it is measurably worse: median anchoring costs $0.21$ of digits density
Spearman, five times the pooled seed spread, and no re-tuning of the level
weights recovers it (\S\ref{sec:ablations}). The cause is that sending the
\emph{median} coarse edge to $0.5$ lifts the bulk of the coarse graph to a
strength it never had, compressing the strong bridges into the same range as
everything else. Anchoring the same map at $q_{0.99}$ keeps the bulk small and
the top percentile distinguished, preserving both the magnitude profile of the
clamp and strict monotonicity. Note that $q_{0.99}\mapsto 0.5$, so the level
weights transfer between \code{quantile\_clamp} and \code{rational\_q99} only
approximately; they do not transfer at all to \code{rational}.
\end{remark}

\paragraph{Bridging a disconnected coarse level.}
If the coarsest level is disconnected, exactly $n_{\mathrm{comp}}-1$ bridge
edges of fixed weight $\code{pyramid\_coarse\_backbone}$ are added, chosen by a
Kruskal pass over representative distances seeded with the existing components.
Bridges only ever join \emph{different} components, so no existing weight is
modified. An earlier version laid a full spanning tree over all representatives
and max-merged it, which overwrote hundreds of existing edges on a level whose
median weight is $\approx 0.02$ and imposed an arbitrary tree geometry on the
global layout; the restriction to inter-component edges is the fix.

\subsection{Multi-scale sampling budget}
\label{sec:budget}

During training the per-step edge budget $B_e$ is divided across the active
levels in proportion to \code{pyramid\_level\_weights}
$=(w_0,\ldots,w_{L_{\mathrm{pyr}}-1})$, finest first:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:budget}
B_\ell
=
\mathrm{round}\!\left(B_e\,\frac{w_\ell}{\sum_{\ell'\in A}w_{\ell'}}\right),
\qquad \ell\in A,
\end{equation}
with the finest active level absorbing the rounding residual so the total is
exactly $B_e$. Sampled positive weights from level $\ell$ are additionally
multiplied by $w_\ell$, so a level's weight controls both how many of its edges
are seen and how hard each pulls. If more weights are supplied than levels were
built, entries are dropped from the \emph{middle} and the coarsest weight
retained, since truncation would remove exactly the long-range attraction the
schedule exists to provide.

\paragraph{Optional coarse-to-fine schedule.}
Setting $\code{coarse\_first\_frac}=c>0$ spends the first $\lceil cE\rceil$
epochs climbing the pyramid, starting with the coarsest level alone and
admitting one finer level at a time; thereafter all levels run together. Steps
per epoch are set by the finest \emph{active} level's edge count,
$\lceil\abs{E_{\min A}}/B_e\rceil$, which is where the saving comes from: an
epoch touching only coarse levels needs roughly $\varrho$ times fewer steps per
coarsening. The rationale is that early epochs decide global layout and coarse
edges already carry it, so paying fine-graph prices for them is waste. The
default $c=0$ reproduces the flat schedule against which every recipe in
\S\ref{sec:results} was tuned.

\subsection{Landmark affinity}
\label{sec:affinity}

\begin{definition}[Softmax landmark affinity]
\label{def:affinity}
With learnable per-landmark temperatures
$\tau_\ell = \tau_{\min}+e^{\theta_\ell}$, $\tau_{\min}=10^{-3}$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:affinity}
D_{i\ell}=d_X(x_i,M_\ell),
\qquad
a_{i\ell}
=
\frac{\exp(-D_{i\ell}/\tau_\ell)}
     {\sum_{\ell'}\exp(-D_{i\ell'}/\tau_{\ell'})}.
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

Temperatures are initialised to the mean distance from landmark $\ell$ to its
$\min(32,L-1)$ nearest landmarks, times \code{tau\_scale}; for scalar views the
anchors are placed at quantile knots rather than by farthest-point sampling.
Landmark coordinates are learnable when the metric declares differentiability
in its second argument, and are frozen with a warning otherwise.

The natural diagnostic is the affinity perplexity
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:perplexity}
\mathrm{perp}_i = \exp\!\left(-\sum_\ell a_{i\ell}\log a_{i\ell}\right)
= \exp H(a_i),
\end{equation}
which is monotone in $\tau$ and interpolates between a hard Voronoi assignment
($\mathrm{perp}\to 1$) and an uninformative uniform code
($\mathrm{perp}\to L$). A calibration utility bisects \code{tau\_scale} so that
the median perplexity meets a target, by default $8$, matching the top-$c$
shortlist width. Both the target and the achieved median belong in
$\mathcal{D}$: a map whose conditioning code is near-uniform is, for all
practical purposes, an unconditioned MLP, and \eqref{eq:perplexity} is the only
thing that reveals it.

\subsection{FiLM backbone}
\label{sec:film}

Let $x_n=(x-\mu)/\sigma$ denote the encoder-normalised input, with $\mu,\sigma$
computed on the training split. A depth-$K$, width-$W$ MLP applies, for
$k=1,\ldots,K$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:film}
h_0 = x_n,
\qquad
\tilde h_k = W_k h_{k-1}\ \ \text{(bias-free)},
\qquad
h_k = \GELU\!\bigl(\gamma_k\odot\LN(\tilde h_k)+\beta_k\bigr),
\end{equation}
with $(\gamma_k,\beta_k)$ produced from the affinity code by per-factor
hypernetworks (\S\ref{sec:roles}). The embedding is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:z}
z =
\begin{cases}
W_{\mathrm{PCA}}\,x_n + W_{\mathrm{head}}h_K + b, & \code{pca\_skip}=\text{True},\\[3pt]
W_{\mathrm{head}}h_K + b, & \text{otherwise.}
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
When the skip is enabled, $W_{\mathrm{PCA}}$ holds the top-$d$ right singular
vectors of the centred normalised training matrix and the residual head is
initialised with standard deviation $10^{-4}$ and zero bias, so training \emph{begins at
plain PCA}.

\begin{proposition}[Modulation must follow normalisation]
\label{prop:filmorder}
Let $\LN(h)=\omega\odot(h-\mu_h)/\varsigma_h+\varpi$ be layer normalisation with
its usual elementwise affine. Then $\LN(ch)=\LN(h)$ for every scalar $c>0$.
Consequently, in the ordering $\GELU(\LN(\gamma\odot\tilde h+\beta))$ a
\emph{per-layer scalar} $\gamma$ has no effect on the forward pass whatsoever,
and a factor emitting only such a scalar contributes nothing while still
receiving gradient noise. In the ordering of~\eqref{eq:film} it acts as
intended.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$\mu_{ch}=c\mu_h$ and $\varsigma_{ch}=c\varsigma_h$ for $c>0$, so
$(ch-\mu_{ch})/\varsigma_{ch}=(h-\mu_h)/\varsigma_h$. Substituting
$\gamma\odot\tilde h=\gamma\tilde h$ for scalar $\gamma>0$ gives the claim; a
scalar $\gamma$ combined with $\beta$ survives only through the ratio
$\abs{\gamma\tilde h}:\abs{\beta}$, not through its own scale.
\end{proof}

Per-channel $\gamma$ is unaffected either way, because a per-channel rescale
changes the cross-channel pattern that $\LN$ normalises. This is the reason the
default (per-channel primary) path is insensitive to the ordering while the
scalar roles are not; results predating the correction are not comparable
across it for those roles.

\subsection{Factor roles and composition}
\label{sec:roles}

Multiple conditioning factors may be stacked, each with its own view, metric,
anchor set, and \emph{role}:

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{lccl}
\toprule
Role & $\gamma^{(f)}$ & $\beta^{(f)}$ & Intended use \\
\midrule
\code{PRIMARY} & per-channel $(B,K,W)$ & per-channel & the main geometry \\
\code{MODULATOR} & per-layer scalar & per-channel & a secondary condition \\
\code{GAIN} & per-layer scalar & --- & a pure strength knob \\
\code{AXIS} & --- & --- & monotone contribution to one output coordinate \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Each non-\code{AXIS} factor owns a two-layer hypernetwork
$\R^{L_f}\to\R^{128}\to\R^{\mathrm{out}(f)}$ whose final layer is
zero-initialised, so every factor begins at the identity modulation
$\gamma=1,\beta=0$ and departs from it only as the data require. Composition is
multiplicative in scale and additive in shift:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:filmcompose}
\gamma
=
\clamp\!\left(\prod_f\gamma^{(f)},\;\gamma_{\min},\;\gamma_{\max}\right),
\qquad
\beta = \sum_f \beta^{(f)},
\end{equation}
with $\gamma_{\min}=0.1$, $\gamma_{\max}=10$. The clamp \emph{hit rate} --- the
fraction of $(B,K,W)$ entries saturating either bound --- is monitored as an
extrapolation diagnostic and belongs in $\mathcal{D}$: a rising hit rate on new
data means the conditioning code is being asked for modulations outside the
range training ever produced.

An \code{AXIS} factor bypasses FiLM entirely and adds a monotone scalar to a
designated output coordinate,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:axis}
\eta(v)
=
\softplus(W_3)^\top\,\psi_2\bigl(\psi_1(v)\bigr) + b_3,
\qquad
\psi_j(u)=\softplus\!\left(\softplus(W_j)^\top u + b_j\right),
\end{equation}
where non-negativity of the weights and monotonicity of $\softplus$ make
$\eta$ non-decreasing in $v$ by construction. This is how a scalar property ---
a temperature, a maximum dimension, a log-magnitude --- becomes an
\emph{interpretable axis} of the map: a reader can state, as a property of the
artefact rather than an observation about one figure, that moving right along
that coordinate never decreases the property.

\subsection{The conditioning control}
\label{sec:concat}

The affinity $a(x)$ is a deterministic function of $x$. FiLM therefore adds no
information the encoder could not compute for itself; it supplies an inductive
bias --- a soft partition-of-unity mixture of experts --- and not capacity. The
honest control is an otherwise identical network of the same width, depth, head
and skip structure with $a(x)$ simply \emph{concatenated} to the normalised
input, $h_0=[x_n,\,a(x)]$. This control is shipped as
$\code{conditioning}=\code{"concat"}$ and is exercised in
\S\ref{sec:filmvsconcat}; its result is narrower than the architecture's
prominence would suggest and is reported as measured.

\subsection{Parameter groups}
\label{sec:paramgroups}

With the PCA skip enabled, a single learning rate couples two unrelated
decisions: how fast the linear skip drifts from its PCA seed, and how fast the
residual head builds structure on top of it. Raise it and the unconstrained
linear path dominates the layout; lower it and the near-zero head never
departs from PCA. The escape is a parameter group, not a compromise rate: the
skip, backbone and normalisation layers train at $\mathrm{lr}$ while the head
and hypernetworks train at $\code{pca\_lr\_mult}\times\mathrm{lr}$. A
multiplier of $1$ reproduces flat-rate behaviour exactly and is the default.
The alternative --- disabling the skip and raising $\mathrm{lr}$ to
$\approx 2\times 10^{-2}$ --- is the shipped small-$N$ recipe. These are one
decision with three settings, not three knobs (\S\ref{sec:defaults}).

%==============================================================================
\section{Training objective}
\label{sec:loss}
%==============================================================================

The per-step objective is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:total}
\begin{aligned}
\loss(t)
={}& \loss_{\mathrm{geom}}
   + \lambda_{\mathrm{ord}}\,\loss_{\mathrm{ord}}
   + \lambda_{\mathrm{lm}}\,\loss_{\mathrm{lm}} \\
   &+ \lambda_{\mathrm{frame}}\,r_{\mathrm{frame}}(t)\,\loss_{\mathrm{frame}}
   + \lambda_{\mathrm{geo}}\,r_{\mathrm{geo}}(t)\,\loss_{\mathrm{geo}}
   + \lambda_{\mathrm{dens}}\,r_{\mathrm{dens}}(t)\,\loss_{\mathrm{dens}},
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
with $\lambda_{\mathrm{ord}}=0.1$ fixed, $t$ the training fraction, and ramps
$r_\cdot$ defined in \S\ref{sec:ramps}. The geodesic term is itself a
combination,
$\loss_{\mathrm{geo}}=\lambda_{\mathrm{anchor}}\loss_{\mathrm{anchor}}
+0.25\,\loss_{\mathrm{stress}}$.

Every term other than $\loss_{\mathrm{geom}}$ and $\loss_{\mathrm{lm}}$ is
\emph{scale-free} by construction: each fits and detaches a global scale so
that no regulariser can dictate the overall size of the embedding. This is a
deliberate invariant, and the reason an earlier one-sided Lipschitz penalty
$\E[(\norm{\partial z/\partial x}_F-1)_+^2]$ was deleted rather than retuned ---
a penalty anchored to a fixed Jacobian norm silently fixes the embedding scale,
contradicting every other term.

\subsection{Low-dimensional kernel and \texorpdfstring{\code{min\_dist}}{min\_dist}}
\label{sec:ab}

As in UMAP, the embedding-space similarity is a rational kernel whose
parameters are fitted to a piecewise target. On a grid of $300$ points
$x\in[0,3\,\mathrm{spread}]$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:target}
y(x)
=
\begin{cases}
1, & x<\code{min\_dist},\\
\exp\bigl(-(x-\code{min\_dist})/\mathrm{spread}\bigr), & x\ge\code{min\_dist},
\end{cases}
\qquad
\hat y(x)=\frac{1}{1+a\,x^{2b}},
\end{equation}
fitted by bounded least squares from $(a,b)=(1,1)$, with fallback
$(1.577,0.895)$ (UMAP's values at $\code{min\_dist}=0.1$). The kernel used in
the geometric loss is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:q}
q(u,v) = \bigl(1 + a\,(\norm{u-v}^2+10^{-10})^{b}\bigr)^{-1},
\end{equation}
clamped to $[10^{-7},1-10^{-7}]$ before logarithms.

\begin{proposition}[Relative attraction near contact]
\label{prop:bstab}
Let $r=\norm{z_i-z_j}$. The attractive force derived from $-\log q$ scales as
$2ab\,r^{2b-1}$ as $r\to0$. The force \emph{per unit separation} therefore
scales as $r^{2b-2}$, which diverges whenever $b<1$: a pair already close is
pulled proportionally harder.
\end{proposition}

This positive feedback is the mechanism that collapses neighbourhoods into
knots separated by voids, and it is a statement about the ratio of force to
separation, not about the force itself --- the force $2ab\,r^{2b-1}$ vanishes
sublinearly for $\tfrac12<b<1$ and there is nothing for automatic
differentiation to overflow on. (What diverges in UMAP's optimiser is the
\emph{repulsive} gradient, whose $1/(\epsilon+r^2)$ factor grows without bound;
that is what its gradient clipping addresses, and it is a property of the
repulsive term rather than of clipped versus exact differentiation.) The
practical consequence is a monotone relationship $\code{min\_dist}\mapsto b$
that is exercised empirically in \S\ref{sec:mindist} rather than converted into
a threshold; a bisection utility reports the smallest \code{min\_dist} reaching
a target $b$ ($\approx 0.199$ for $b=1$ at unit spread).

\subsection{Fuzzy cross-entropy}
\label{sec:geom}

Positive edges are drawn with probability proportional to
$P^{\mathrm{sym}}_{ij}$ by Vose alias sampling, then expanded from
$\varepsilon$-net cells to uniformly chosen raw members, so that duplicate mass
is represented in the batch without being represented in the graph. Negatives
are drawn uniformly over representatives and likewise cell-expanded. With
$n_{\mathrm{neg}}=5$ by default,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:geom}
\loss_{\mathrm{geom}}
=
-\frac{1}{B}\sum_{(i,j)\in E_B} w_{ij}\log q(z_i,z_j)
-\frac{1}{B\,n_{\mathrm{neg}}}\sum_{i}\sum_{m=1}^{n_{\mathrm{neg}}}
\log\bigl(1-q(z_i,z^-_{i,m})\bigr).
\end{equation}
Unlike UMAP's asynchronous clipped-force SGD, this is differentiated directly,
which is what makes (R5) hold and what makes $b$ a genuine stability parameter
rather than an artefact absorbed by clipping.

\subsection{Ordinal triplets and their measured null}
\label{sec:ord}

The affinity code carries an ordering that the geometric term never sees: which
landmark a point is closest to, second closest to, and so on. A weak ordinal
term exploits it. For quadruples (anchor, near, mid, far) verified to satisfy
$d_{\mathrm{an}}<d_{\mathrm{am}}<d_{\mathrm{af}}$ in the factor's view metric,
with an adaptive scale $s\leftarrow 0.9s+0.1\,\overline{d_{\mathrm{af}}}$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ord}
\loss_{\mathrm{ord}}
=
\E\!\left[
-\log\varsigma\!\left(\frac{d_{\mathrm{am}}-d_{\mathrm{an}}}{s}\right)
-\log\varsigma\!\left(\frac{d_{\mathrm{af}}-d_{\mathrm{am}}}{s}\right)
\right],
\end{equation}
$\varsigma$ the logistic function. Mid points are drawn from the bucket of the
anchor's rank-$r$ landmark with $r$ log-uniform on $[2,L]$; far points are
uniform.

\paragraph{Retention needs a null, and the null is data-dependent.}
The fraction of proposed triplets surviving verification is interpretable only
against the rate at which a triplet would pass with \emph{no} information in
the landmark ranking. That rate is not a universal constant: it depends on the
sampler, on the bucket-size distribution, and on the data. It is therefore
\emph{measured} at fit time by permuting each row's landmark ordering and
re-running the identical sampler --- which destroys the rank--distance
association while leaving the rank draw, bucket-based mid selection, and
uniform far point untouched. Measured values are $0.472$ (iris), $0.484$ (swiss
roll) and $0.487$ (digits): close to one another, but obtained rather than
assumed, and correct by construction if the sampling scheme changes. The
warning threshold floats with the measured null. The margin is narrow --- a
retention of $0.55$ sits only $\approx 0.07$ above chance --- so values in the
$0.5$--$0.6$ band should be read as barely informative rather than as a pass.
This is a small example of a general principle we apply throughout: a
diagnostic without its null is not a diagnostic.

\subsection{Landmark regularisation}
\label{sec:lm}

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lm}
\loss_{\mathrm{lm}}
=
\frac{1}{B}\sum_i\sum_\ell a_{i\ell}D_{i\ell}
\;-\;
\eta\sum_\ell \bar a_\ell\log\bar a_\ell,
\qquad
\bar a_\ell=\frac{1}{B}\sum_i a_{i\ell},
\end{equation}
with $\eta=1$. The first term is soft quantisation error, pulling landmarks
toward the mass they are responsible for; the second is a load-balancing
entropy over \emph{mean} affinities, penalising the collapse in which a few
landmarks absorb all assignment and the rest become dead code. Because the
landmarks are also the support model (\S\ref{sec:conformal}), dead landmarks
are not merely wasted capacity --- they are holes in the declared domain.

\subsection{Local rigidity in the Gram domain}
\label{sec:frame}

The geometric term constrains \emph{who} is near whom and says nothing about
the shape of a neighbourhood. A map may therefore satisfy it while shearing
local frames relative to one another --- the twist-and-pinch failure that makes
a ribbon appear to fold. The frame term is an as-rigid-as-possible
penalty~\cite{sorkine2007arap} expressed on neighbour Gram matrices.

For a centre $c$ with neighbours $\{k\}$ (a \emph{star}), write ambient offsets
$u_k=x_k-x_c$ and embedding offsets $v_k=z_k-z_c$. A similarity transform
preserves every pairwise inner product up to one scalar, so with
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:grams}
(G_x)_{kl}=\inner{u_k}{u_l},
\qquad
(G_z)_{kl}=\inner{v_k}{v_l},
\qquad
M_{kl}=m_k m_l,
\end{equation}
($m$ the padding/validity mask) we take the per-star detached scale and the
globally normalised residual
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:frame}
s_c^2
=
\frac{\inner{\mathrm{sg}[G_z]}{G_x}_{M}}{\inner{G_x}{G_x}_{M}},
\qquad
\loss_{\mathrm{frame}}
=
\frac{\sum_c\sum_{kl}M_{kl}\bigl((G_z)_{kl}-s_c^2 (G_x)_{kl}\bigr)^2}
     {\sum_c\sum_{kl}M_{kl}\,\mathrm{sg}[(G_z)_{kl}]^2},
\end{equation}
$\mathrm{sg}[\cdot]$ denoting stop-gradient. The diagonal of~\eqref{eq:frame}
recovers edge-length isometry; the off-diagonals encode relative orientation,
so a length-preserving shear is penalised. The objective is invariant to
rotations and reflections and requires no singular value decomposition in the
backward pass, which makes it fully batched and portable to accelerators
lacking an SVD kernel.

\paragraph{Tangent mode.}
On manifolds that fold back on themselves, ambient $k$-NN includes across-sheet
shortcuts whose offsets point off the local surface; enforcing ambient rigidity
then glues the sheets together. With $\code{frame\_tangent}=\text{True}$, under
stop-gradient: (i) distance-weight the offsets by
$w_k=\exp\bigl(-\tfrac12(\norm{u_k}/h)^2\bigr)$ with $h$ the mean neighbour
distance, so that the nearest --- almost always same-sheet --- neighbours
dominate; (ii) take the top-$d_{\mathrm{eff}}$ right singular vectors of the
weighted offset cloud as a local tangent basis $T$; (iii) drop neighbours whose
normal residual fraction $\norm{u-\Pi_T u}/\norm{u}$ exceeds
$\code{FRAME\_NORMAL\_THRESH}=0.5$; (iv) match Grams on the tangent-projected
offsets $u\leftarrow uT$.

In this mode the term is essentially local tangent space
alignment~\cite{zhang2004ltsa}: steps (i)--(ii) are LTSA's weighted local
tangent estimate and step (iv) imposes LTSA's requirement that tangent
coordinate structure agree between data and embedding. It departs from LTSA in
enforcing that agreement on Gram matrices with a detached scale rather than by
aligning coordinates, and in applying it as a penalty on a parametric map
rather than as a global eigenproblem solved for the embedding.

\paragraph{Why the ramp must be delayed on fold-back data.}
Local rigidity cannot distinguish a rolled manifold from its unrolled isometry:
both are locally rigid, and the transition between them is not. The term
therefore penalises the \emph{transient} stretch of unrolling. On such data
$r_{\mathrm{frame}}$ must rise only after the neighbour term has globally
unrolled --- $(0.5,0.75)$ is the measured setting --- after which it cleans up
residual width and twist. On non-folding sheets, early and delayed both help
and delayed remains the safer default.

\subsection{Geodesic gauge}
\label{sec:geo}

Affinity losses leave the global metric gauge free: a smoothly bent
(``banana'') layout can be locally indistinguishable from a straight one. The
geodesic term fixes it, using the landmarks as a coarse backbone.

Each landmark is mapped to its nearest training row; Dijkstra on the
symmetrised $k$-NN distance graph gives landmark--landmark geodesics
$g_{\ell\ell'}$ together with a finiteness mask. Classical
multidimensional scaling~\cite{torgerson1952,isomap2000} produces a
\emph{frozen} target $Y\in\R^{L\times d}$:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:cmds}
J = I-\tfrac1L\bm{1}\bm{1}^\top,
\qquad
B = -\tfrac12\,J\,G^{(2)}\,J,
\qquad
Y = V_d\Lambda_d^{1/2},
\quad \Lambda_d=\max(\lambda_d,0),
\end{equation}
from the eigendecomposition of the symmetrised Gram $B$, with non-finite
entries of $G$ filled by the maximum finite distance.

\begin{remark}[The clamp discards evidence, so the evidence is reported]
\label{rem:negeigen}
$B$ is positive semi-definite only when the geodesics are
Euclidean-embeddable. They frequently are not: a manifold with a hole, a cone,
or one whose shortest paths wrap a fold produces genuinely non-Euclidean $g$,
and the negative eigenvalues of $B$ are precisely the record of that. The clamp
$\max(\lambda,0)$ throws the record away silently, leaving a target $Y$ that
looks unremarkable however badly it fits. The negative mass is therefore
emitted as a first-class diagnostic,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:negeigen}
\nu = \frac{\sum_{\lambda_i<0}\abs{\lambda_i}}{\sum_i\abs{\lambda_i}},
\end{equation}
alongside the fraction of positive spectrum captured by the retained $d$
components. Large $\nu$ means the MDS target is a poor summary of the geodesic
structure and $\loss_{\mathrm{anchor}}$ is pulling toward something the data
does not support. The response is a warning at $\nu>0.10$, not an adjustment:
making $\lambda_{\mathrm{anchor}}$ a hidden function of $\nu$ would mean the
recipe no longer states what the objective is.
\end{remark}

\paragraph{Scale-invariant stress.}
On sampled landmark pairs, with $d_z=\norm{z_a-z_b}$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:stress}
s = \frac{\inner{\mathrm{sg}[d_z]}{g}}{\inner{g}{g}},
\qquad
\loss_{\mathrm{stress}}
=
\frac{\E\bigl[(d_z-s\,g)^2\bigr]}{\E\bigl[\mathrm{sg}[d_z]^2\bigr]}.
\end{equation}
This constrains \emph{how far} landmarks sit from one another, which affinity
losses leave free.

\paragraph{Procrustes anchor.}
Stress is invariant to any local frame twist that approximately preserves
pairwise distances, so it cannot by itself select the untwisted layout. An
absolute-position prior does. Let $Z_M$ be the current landmark embeddings.
Under stop-gradient, align the frozen target $Y$ to $Z_M$ by a similarity:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:procrustes}
\begin{gathered}
\Xi = Y_c^\top Z_{M,c},
\qquad
R = \argmax_{R\in SO(d)}\tr(R^\top\Xi),
\qquad
c_s = \frac{\max_R\tr(R^\top\Xi)}{\norm{Y_c}_F^2},\\
t = \bar z_M - c_s\,\bar Y R,
\qquad
\loss_{\mathrm{anchor}}
=
\frac{1}{Ld}\norm{Z_M-\bigl(c_s YR+t\bigr)}_F^2 .
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
Gradients flow only into $Z_M$, so the term pins the gauge without fighting the
arbitrary rigid motion of the embedding. Note that $R$ is taken over $SO(d)$
rather than $O(d)$: reflections are excluded, so a mirrored layout is
corrected rather than accepted. In $d=2$ the maximiser is closed form,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:rot2d}
\theta^\star = \operatorname{atan2}(\Xi_{10}-\Xi_{01},\ \Xi_{00}+\Xi_{11}),
\qquad
\max_R\tr(R^\top\Xi)=\sqrt{(\Xi_{00}+\Xi_{11})^2+(\Xi_{10}-\Xi_{01})^2},
\end{equation}
which avoids both an SVD and a determinant branch inside the training step ---
a genuine cost on accelerators where the $2\times2$ factorisation falls back to
the host and the branch forces a device synchronisation every step. A vanishing
maximum means the two configurations are uncorrelated, and the scale then goes
to zero, collapsing the target to a point, which is the honest answer rather
than an arbitrary rotation of noise.

Setting $\lambda_{\mathrm{anchor}}=0$ retains scale-invariant stress while
dropping the absolute-position prior. That is the right test when $\nu$ is
large, and it is worth running whenever the frame term is active, since local
rigidity already penalises twist without committing to a global target
(\S\ref{sec:ablations}).

\subsection{Density ordering}
\label{sec:density}

Left unconstrained, which neighbourhoods come out crowded is decided by
wherever the attraction--repulsion equilibrium lands, which is a property of
\code{min\_dist} and not of the data. A map can therefore invent clumps, or
flatten real ones, with nothing in the objective objecting --- an unacceptable
property for a map used to judge whether a region of parameter space is
well-sampled.

An earlier version of this term targeted density \emph{magnitude}. Preserving
mass under a dimension change requires
$\log r_{\mathrm{embed}}=(m/d)\log r_{\mathrm{ambient}}+\text{const}$, so the
term pinned the layout's log-radius spread to $m/d$ times the ambient spread.
That is correct arithmetic and a bad objective: when $m\gg d$ the required
spread exceeds anything an equilibrium can produce. On $64$-dimensional digits
($\hat m\approx 7.3$ into $2$) it demanded $3.6\times$ the ambient spread,
saturated its own \code{min\_dist} search against a ceiling, and degraded class
structure while chasing a number it could never reach.

Correlating log radii instead is scale-free, and the $m/d$ factor drops out
because correlation is invariant to it. This is the shape of objective densMAP
uses~\cite{narayan2021densmap}. Let a \emph{star} be a graph node together with
a \emph{fixed} neighbour list of width $k$ and mask $m_k$, normalised weights
$\hat w_k=m_k/\sum m$. Define
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:logr}
\ell_z(c)
=
\tfrac12\log\sum_k \hat w_k\norm{z_k-z_c}^2,
\qquad
\ell_x(c)
=
\tfrac12\log\sum_k \hat w_k\,d_X(x_k,x_c)^2,
\end{equation}
the log RMS neighbour radius in each space, over \emph{the same} star. Then
with $\tilde\ell=\ell-\bar\ell$ over the batch,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:dens}
\loss_{\mathrm{dens}}
=
1
-
\frac{\frac1n\sum_c\tilde\ell_z(c)\,\tilde\ell_x(c)}
     {\sqrt{\Var[\ell_z]+\varsigma_0}\;\sqrt{\Var[\ell_x]}},
\qquad
\varsigma_0=\code{density\_var\_shift}=0.1 .
\end{equation}

Three details are load-bearing. First, the ambient side $\ell_x$ is tabulated
once before training over the \emph{deterministic} first-$k$ neighbour list, and
the embedded side is measured over that same list, so the two differ only in the
space they were measured in; a fresh random draw each step would leave sampling
noise in the comparison and the optimiser would pay for it by shrinking the
contrast it dares to show. Second, the variance floor $\varsigma_0$ is added
only to the embedded side, so a layout whose radii barely vary has its gradient
damped by the floor rather than amplified by a vanishing denominator --- the
desirable direction to err, since such a layout has no density signal to be
confident about. Third, the term ranges over $[0,2]$ with $1.0$ the score of a
layout whose density is unrelated to the data's, which puts it on the same
footing as the other unit-scale terms; batches with fewer than two stars, or
with ambient standard deviation below $10^{-3}$, return $1.0$ with no gradient.

Being scale-free removes a way to fail but does not make density preservation
free. Sweeping $\lambda_{\mathrm{dens}}$ on digits costs local structure
monotonically ($\mathrm{trust}_5$ $0.947\to0.919$, $k$-NN overlap
$0.395\to0.318$ from $0$ to $2.0$) while on the s-curve the same sweep improves
both at once. That trade-off is a property of the data, not of the
parameterisation: the density signal of a clustered dataset is largely discrete
cluster structure, so honouring it fights the neighbour terms. The mitigation,
also borrowed from densMAP, is a gate: the term is held off until the tail of
training ($\code{density\_ramp}=(0.7,0.7)$, a hard gate at $70\%$) so that it
refines a settled topology rather than steering a forming one.

\subsection{Ramps and optimisation}
\label{sec:ramps}

For training fraction $t$ and breakpoints $(t_0,t_1)$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ramp}
r_\uparrow(t)
=
\begin{cases}
1, & t_0=t_1=0,\\
0, & t<t_0,\\
(t-t_0)/(t_1-t_0), & t_0\le t<t_1,\\
1, & t\ge t_1,
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
with a mirrored ramp-down variant available. Equal breakpoints give a hard
gate. Defaults: $\code{geo\_ramp}=(0.2,0.45)$,
$\code{density\_ramp}=(0.7,0.7)$, $\code{frame\_ramp}=(0,0)$ except on
fold-back data.

Optimisation uses AdamW with weight decay $10^{-4}$, linear warmup over $5\%$ of
total steps from a start factor of $0.01$, then cosine annealing; gradients are
clipped to $\ell_2$ norm $5$ and non-finite steps are skipped and logged. Steps
per epoch follow $\lceil\abs{E_{\mathrm{active}}}/B_e\rceil$
(\S\ref{sec:budget}). A two-phase override holding a constant rate for a fixed
number of epochs before switching is available and disables warmup and cosine.

\subsection{Warm start from the landmark geodesics}
\label{sec:warmstart}

The classical-MDS layout $Y$ of \S\ref{sec:geo} is already computed before
training, and it is a coarse but topologically informed placement of the
manifold. Using it only as a running penalty wastes it: training otherwise
begins either from a linear PCA projection or, with the skip disabled, from a
near-zero head that must build a layout from scratch at a raised learning rate.

Placing every point relative to the landmarks needs no optimisation, only
interpolation. With $c=8$ nearest landmarks and inverse-distance weights,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:nystrom}
\hat z(x)
=
\kappa\sum_{\ell\in\Nb_c(x)}
\frac{d_X(x,M_\ell)^{-p}}{\sum_{\ell'\in\Nb_c(x)}d_X(x,M_{\ell'})^{-p}}\,
Y_\ell,
\qquad p=2,
\end{equation}
the Nystr\"om out-of-sample extension of landmark MDS, with distances floored
at $10^{-8}$ so a point coincident with a landmark takes that landmark's
coordinate. The global factor $\kappa$ is chosen so that the median
nearest-neighbour distance of $\{\hat z\}$ equals \code{min\_dist}. That
rescaling matters: $Y$ carries geodesic units unrelated to the scale at which
the attraction--repulsion equilibrium settles, so regressing onto raw MDS
coordinates would hand the layout a correct shape at the wrong size and make
the first phase of training an argument about scale.

\begin{remark}[The obvious kernel is the wrong one]
\label{rem:nystromkernel}
The FiLM affinity $a(x)$ looks like the natural interpolation kernel --- it is
already a softmax partition of unity over exactly these landmarks --- but at
the calibrated temperature it is deliberately \emph{not} local: on digits, $124$
of $128$ landmarks are effective for the median point, so $a(x)^\top Y$
collapses toward the centroid and carries almost no local structure
($\mathrm{trust}_5$ $0.823$ against PCA's $0.830$, i.e.\ no better than the
initialisation it was meant to replace). Inverse-distance weights over the $c$
nearest landmarks beat PCA on every dataset measured --- $\mathrm{trust}_5$
$0.995$ against $0.977$ and $k$-NN overlap $0.552$ against $0.311$ on swiss
roll, $0.999/0.718$ against $0.981/0.279$ on the s-curve. On the s-curve, that
interpolation alone reaches the \emph{fully trained} neighbour overlap
($0.718$ against $0.725$) before a single gradient step.
\end{remark}

Three coarse layouts are candidates: \code{isomap} (the landmark MDS above),
\code{spectral} (leading eigenvectors of the normalised fuzzy adjacency,
UMAP's initialisation, computed as the top eigenvectors of
$D^{-1/2}WD^{-1/2}+2I$ with the stationary vector dropped and signs pinned by
the largest-magnitude entry for reproducibility), and \code{pca}. Neither of
the first two dominates: Isomap wins on manifolds that are genuinely a sheet,
spectral wins when the geodesics are not realisable in $d$ dimensions and the
MDS spectrum goes negative, while on an elongated domain the leading
eigenvectors are successive harmonics of the same direction and the layout
degenerates to a curve. Rather than predicting the winner from $\nu$, the
\code{auto} setting \emph{measures} it: each candidate is interpolated onto the
graph's representatives by~\eqref{eq:nystrom} and scored by neighbour agreement
against the $k$-NN the build already computed --- the same quantity the layouts
are being chosen for, at a cost of one KD-tree query per candidate. If
\code{pca} wins, the coarse layouts had nothing to add and the regression is
skipped rather than spent teaching the encoder something PCA already gives it.

The regression itself is plain: minimise $\norm{f_\theta(x)-\hat z(x)}^2$ over
encoder parameters only, with landmarks and temperatures frozen (the targets
were computed from them, so letting them move would make the objective chase
its own tail). One regression step costs roughly a ninth of a training step ---
one forward pass, no negatives, no triplets, no graph sampling.

%==============================================================================
\section{Support model, calibration, and repair}
\label{sec:conformal}
%==============================================================================

Requirement R4 asks the artefact to state where it is valid and with what error
rate. This section builds that statement in three layers: a geometric support
model, a distribution-free calibration of it, and a taxonomy that turns a
binary flag into a typed one.

\subsection{From cover to tangent charts}
\label{sec:cover}

The baseline score is the \emph{landmark cover},
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:cover}
\mathrm{cover}(x) = \min_\ell \frac{\phi(x,M_\ell)}{s_{\mathrm{nat}}},
\end{equation}
the distance to the nearest landmark in units of the natural scale. It is
cheap --- it is already computed by the forward pass --- and it is what
$\code{embed()}$ returns alongside the coordinates. It has two defects, and the
implementation fixes them in order.

\paragraph{A single global scale is the wrong denominator.}
With one $s_{\mathrm{nat}}$, \eqref{eq:cover} is a statement about absolute
distance, so the sparse tail of the training distribution is flagged as readily
as anything genuinely off-manifold. Replacing the global scale by a local one,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:covlocal}
\mathrm{cover}(x)=\min_\ell\frac{\phi(x,M_\ell)}{r_\ell},
\qquad
r_\ell=\med\bigl\{\phi(x_i,M_\ell)\,:\,\ell(x_i)=\ell,\ x_i\in\text{train}\bigr\},
\end{equation}
buys approximate \emph{conditional} validity while keeping a single pooled
calibration set: a typical point scores $\approx1$ in a dense and in a sparse
region alike.

\paragraph{Isotropic balls are exponentially too generous in high dimension.}
The acceptance region $\{\mathrm{cover}\le\tau\}$ is a union of $L$ balls of
volume $\sim\tau^D$ each, whereas the support near a landmark is a sheet of
extent $\tau$ in $m$ tangent directions and thickness $t\ll\tau$ in the
remaining $D-m$, i.e.\ volume $\sim\tau^m t^{D-m}$. The ratio is
$(\tau/t)^{D-m}$. Splitting each neighbourhood by a local principal-component
chart replaces balls with tangent-aligned ellipsoids. Let $V_\ell\in\R^{D\times
m}$ be the top-$m$ right singular vectors of the \emph{centred} training
scatter in bucket $\ell$, and for $u=x-M_\ell$ write $u_\parallel=V_\ell^\top
u$, $u_\perp = u - V_\ell u_\parallel$. With $\sigma^\parallel_\ell,
\sigma^\perp_\ell$ the median training magnitudes of each part,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:chartscore}
s(x)
=
\min_\ell
\sqrt{
\frac{\norm{u_\parallel}^2}{(\sigma^\parallel_\ell)^2}
+
\frac{\norm{u_\perp}^2}{(\sigma^\perp_\ell)^2}
} .
\end{equation}
This is the \emph{default} score; \eqref{eq:covlocal} is the fallback and is
recovered exactly by setting $V_\ell=0$ and
$\sigma^\parallel_\ell=\sigma^\perp_\ell=r_\ell$, which is what buckets with
fewer than $\max(8,m+2)$ training points do automatically. All scales are
floored at $10^{-3}$ of the global median owned distance, so a degenerate
direction cannot produce an infinite score. Implementation note: $\norm{u_\perp}^2$
is obtained as $\norm{u}^2-\norm{u_\parallel}^2$, which avoids materialising a
$(B,L,D)$ tensor.

Measured on a two-dimensional sheet embedded in $\R^8$, the ratio between the
score of an off-sheet displacement and an along-sheet displacement of equal
length is:

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{lcc}
\toprule
sheet thickness $t$ & locally scaled balls & tangent charts \\
\midrule
$0.05$  & 1.34 & 2.3 \\
$0.02$  & 1.35 & 4.3 \\
$0.005$ & 1.34 & 14.9 \\
$0.001$ & 1.34 & 74.2 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Balls are indifferent to thickness --- the discrimination sits at $1.34$
regardless --- while the chart score scales as $1/t$. This is the
$(\tau/t)^{D-m}$ argument appearing as a measurable quantity rather than an
asymptotic remark. Charts are linear and therefore assume the primary view is a
vector space; for a non-linear view, the ball mode is correct.

\subsection{Conformal calibration}
\label{sec:pvalue}

Fix a nonconformity score $s$ before calibration and let
$s_{(1)}\le\cdots\le s_{(n)}$ be its values on a held-out calibration split.
For a test point,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:pvalue}
p(x) = \frac{1+\#\{j: s_{(j)}\ge s(x)\}}{n+1},
\end{equation}
computed by binary search. Under exchangeability of the calibration and test
scores, $p$ is super-uniform, so flagging $p\le\alpha$ controls the in-support
false-alarm rate at level $\alpha$~\cite{vovk2005}. The resolution floor is
$1/(n+1)$: levels below it are unreachable and the implementation warns when
$n<200$. Equivalently, the one-sided threshold is the order statistic
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:thresh}
q(\alpha)=s_{(k)},
\qquad
k=\lceil (n+1)(1-\alpha)\rceil,
\end{equation}
returning $+\infty$ when $k>n$, which is the honest answer that no finite
threshold attains the requested level with this much calibration data.

Batch-level shift is tested separately by a permutation Mann--Whitney statistic
against the calibration scores, with Benjamini--Hochberg control across
multiple batches. Calibration is bound to the model by a hash of its parameters;
scoring with weights that do not match raises rather than silently returning
invalid $p$-values.

\begin{remark}[What may be estimated on all of $X$, and why]
\label{rem:validity}
Conformal validity requires the score function to be fixed before calibration
and the calibration scores to be exchangeable with the test scores. Two
different arguments apply here, and conflating them invites a spurious leak
report. The natural scale $s_{\mathrm{nat}}$ and any global $(\mu,\sigma)$
standardisation are \emph{strictly monotone rescalings} applied identically to
calibration and test scores; \eqref{eq:pvalue} depends on the scores only
through the count $\#\{s_j\ge s(x)\}$, which is invariant under any common
increasing map, so they may be estimated on all of $X$ with no effect
whatsoever on the $p$-values. The per-landmark radii and charts of
\eqref{eq:covlocal}--\eqref{eq:chartscore} are \emph{not} rank-preserving ---
they deliberately reorder points --- so the same argument does not cover them;
they are instead fitted on the \emph{training} split, which is disjoint from
calibration by construction, since the calibration split is taken from raw $X$
before landmark selection. Both are sound, for different reasons; only the
second needs the split.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Per-landmark Mondrian is not viable at these shapes]
\label{rem:mondrianbucket}
Pooling calibration \emph{per landmark bucket} would give conditional coverage
directly, and cannot be done here: with $L\sim 64$--$256$ landmarks against
$n_{\mathrm{cal}}\lesssim 200$--$2000$, most buckets receive zero or one
calibration point and a per-bucket $p$-value has resolution $1/(n_\ell+1)$. The
radii and charts are fitted from \emph{training} points, which are plentiful,
so they do not hit the same wall. The asymmetry is deliberate: condition the
\emph{score} on the bucket, keep the \emph{calibration} pooled.
\end{remark}

\subsection{A second, complementary coordinate}
\label{sec:entropy}

The cover is an ambient statement and can be fooled: a point can sit close to a
landmark while being incoherent with respect to the chart structure around it.
The affinity entropy
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:aent}
H(a)=-\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}a_\ell\log a_\ell
\end{equation}
is a second, cheap statistic of the same forward pass, large when assignment
mass is spread across many landmarks and small when it is peaked. Empirically
on digits the two are only moderately rank-correlated (Spearman $\approx 0.5$
across groups, $\approx 0.35$ within real digits), so they carry complementary
information: cover orders the families along ambient distance
($\mathrm{digit}\ll\mathrm{gauss}\ll\mathrm{shuffle}$) while entropy flags flat
affinity irrespective of distance.

Because they are complementary and both cheap, the recommended score combines
them by a supervised linear rule. Given $\varphi(x)=(\mathrm{cover}(x),H(a(x)))
\in\R^2$, an in-support pool and a synthetic out-of-distribution pool, Fisher
linear discriminant analysis gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lda}
S_w = n_0\widehat\Sigma_0+n_1\widehat\Sigma_1+10^{-6}I,
\qquad
w \propto S_w^{-1}(\mu_1-\mu_0),
\qquad
b = -w^\top\frac{\mu_0+\mu_1}{2},
\end{equation}
with $w$ normalised to unit length and the sign oriented so the
out-of-distribution mean scores higher, yielding
$s_{\mathrm{LDA}}(x)=w^\top\varphi(x)+b$. This is a fixed nonconformity once
$(w,b)$ are fitted on the training split; calibration must then use a disjoint
in-support holdout. On a clean digits model the learned normal balances both
axes, $w\approx(0.70,0.71)$.

\subsection{Mondrian categories: typed anomalies}
\label{sec:mondrian}

A single flag says only ``unusual''. For triage, what is wanted is
\emph{which kind} of unusual --- and that is a question a taxonomy can answer
without labels, because the plausible failure families can be synthesised from
the data itself. The shipped taxonomy is
$\mathcal{G}=\{\text{real},\text{gauss},\text{shuffle}\}$: held-out real points;
independent Gaussian noise matched to the per-feature mean and standard
deviation of the real pool; and feature-permuted copies of real rows, which
preserve the exact intensity multiset while destroying all structure. The
second isolates \emph{marginal} plausibility, the third \emph{joint} structure,
and a score that separates real from both is doing something more specific than
detecting scale.

For each group $g$, calibrate separately and take group-conditional
thresholds~\eqref{eq:thresh}. Prediction sets use \emph{two-sided} rank
$p$-values,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:twosided}
p_g(x) = \min\!\left(1,\;
2\min\!\bigl(p^{\mathrm{lo}}_g(x),\,p^{\mathrm{hi}}_g(x)\bigr)\right),
\qquad
p^{\mathrm{lo}}_g = \frac{1+\#\{s^{(g)}_j\le s\}}{n_g+1},
\end{equation}
and the prediction set at level $\alpha$ is $\{g: p_g(x)>\alpha\}$. The
two-sidedness is essential here and is a point that upper-tailed conformal
practice obscures: a typical real point is not in the upper tail of the
Gaussian pool, but it is also not \emph{typical of} the Gaussian pool, and only
a two-sided rank test says so. A point accepted by exactly one group is a
confident typed call; the empty set means every calibrated category rejects it,
which is itself informative --- it is a new kind of anomaly.

\subsection{Closed-form minimum-norm repair}
\label{sec:repair}

An artefact that can reject an input is more useful if it can also say what
would have been accepted. Under the ball score the acceptance region is a union
$\bigcup_\ell B(M_\ell,\tau r_\ell)$, and projection onto a union of balls is
projection onto \emph{one} of them --- but, and this is easy to get wrong, not
in general the one with the nearest centre. The travel required to reach ball
$\ell$ is $d_\ell-\tau r_\ell$, so the correct target is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:repairstar}
\ell^\star = \argmin_\ell\bigl(d_\ell-\tau r_\ell\bigr),
\qquad d_\ell=\phi(x,M_\ell),
\end{equation}
which coincides with $\argmin_\ell d_\ell$ only when all radii are equal ---
and with per-landmark radii they never are, by design. A farther landmark with
a more generous radius can be the cheaper repair. Given $\ell^\star$ the move is
closed form,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:repair}
x_{\mathrm{rep}}
=
\frac{\tau r_{\ell^\star}}{d_{\ell^\star}}\,x
+
\left(1-\frac{\tau r_{\ell^\star}}{d_{\ell^\star}}\right)M_{\ell^\star},
\end{equation}
a convex combination, and a no-op when $x$ is already inside. Under the chart
score the target is selected the same way and the point is moved onto that
landmark's isotropic ball of radius $\tau r_{\ell^\star}$ --- a conservative
inner move, since the exact ellipsoid projection reduces to a two-dimensional
problem in $(\norm{\delta_\parallel},\norm{\delta_\perp})$ and is not needed to
certify membership.

The certificate is honest about its own looseness. Repaired probes pass the
conformal test by construction, and remain roughly twice as far from real
digits under an independent transport metric as a genuine held-out digit is
(\S\ref{sec:emd}). Landmarks are a coarse support model; tightening $\alpha$
cannot fix a shape mismatch. The repair says ``this is the cheapest way to
enter the region the artefact certifies'', not ``this is now a realistic
datum''.

\subsection{Amortised distance-to-support}
\label{sec:negspace}

The cover and chart scores are geometric summaries against $L$ landmarks. A
finer, though model-dependent, statement is available by treating the frozen
encoder as a learned probe. Freeze $f_\theta$, push \emph{perturbed} points
through it, and capture internal state: landmark distances and affinities, the
FiLM $\gamma$ and $\beta$, the post-activation hidden layers, and the embedding.
Fit a quantile-regression head on those features predicting the empirical
distance-to-support $y(\tilde x)=\min_j\norm{\tilde x - x_j}$ over the training
set.

Training points are generated by a fixed perturbation scheme: base points drawn
with replacement from the training set, isotropic shells at radii log-spaced
over $[0.25,25]$ times the median $1$-NN distance, optional on-manifold points
at $r=0$, and uniform coverage of a data box padded by $50\%$ of each
dimension's range. Two draws with the same configuration and different seeds
are exchangeable, which is what makes a valid conformalised holdout available.

The head predicts a median and two non-negative offsets, so quantiles cannot
cross by construction:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:qhead}
q_{\mathrm{med}} = \softplus(g_{\mathrm{med}}),
\quad
q_{\mathrm{lo}} = \max\!\bigl(q_{\mathrm{med}}-\softplus(g_{\mathrm{lo}}),0\bigr),
\quad
q_{\mathrm{hi}} = q_{\mathrm{med}}+\softplus(g_{\mathrm{hi}}),
\end{equation}
trained with pinball losses
$\rho_\tau(e)=\max(\tau e,(\tau-1)e)$ at $\tau\in\{\alpha/2,0.5,1-\alpha/2\}$,
with the median optionally detached in the offset branches so that it is shaped
only by its own $0.5$-pinball term and the offsets shape only the width.
Conformalised quantile regression~\cite{romano2019cqr} then widens (or, when
the base quantiles over-cover, tightens) the interval by
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:cqr}
\Delta
=
Q_{(1-\alpha)(1+1/n)}\Bigl(\bigl\{\max(q_{\mathrm{lo}}-y,\;y-q_{\mathrm{hi}})\bigr\}\Bigr),
\end{equation}
giving finite-sample marginal coverage of the distance-to-support. For pure
novelty detection only the on-manifold null is calibrated --- the raw median
head output on real points --- which keeps the test valid under covariate shift
of the negatives.

\begin{remark}[Why map-space AUROC of unstructured probes misleads]
\label{rem:hull}
It is tempting to score a map's novelty behaviour by how far new points land
from training points \emph{in the embedding}. For an interpolating transform
this measures the wrong thing. \code{UMAP.transform} places a new point as a
weighted mean of training neighbours' embedding coordinates --- a convex
combination --- so it cannot emit coordinates outside the convex hull of the
training map however unlike the input is. Detectability of unstructured probes
is then largely about how much empty space a layout reserves in its interior,
not about representing ``far away''. \S\ref{sec:novelty} measures both and
reports them separately for exactly this reason: it is the \emph{ambient} score
that should gate, and the map that should be looked at.
\end{remark}

%==============================================================================
\section{Evaluation protocol}
\label{sec:eval}
%==============================================================================

A map is judged on several axes that trade against one another, and a single
headline number for any of them is easy to game. This section states the
battery, the nulls, and --- most importantly for R6 --- the persistence
protocol, which measures a property none of the standard metrics touch.

\subsection{Standard fidelity battery}
\label{sec:battery}

\paragraph{Shepard statistics.}
For paired distances $(g_i,e_i)$ --- ambient or geodesic against embedding ---
fit an isotropic scale and report the normalised residual,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:shepard}
\alpha=\frac{\inner{g}{e}}{\inner{e}{e}},
\qquad
\mathrm{stress}=\sqrt{\frac{\sum_i(\alpha e_i-g_i)^2}{\sum_i g_i^2}},
\end{equation}
together with the Spearman rank correlation. Ambient Shepard uses random
distinct pairs; geodesic Shepard uses Dijkstra on the fuzzy graph with hop
length $1/\max(w,10^{-6})$.

\paragraph{Trustworthiness and continuity.}
With ambient ranks $r^X_{ij}$ and embedding neighbour sets $\Nb^Z_k$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:trust}
T_k
=
1-\frac{2}{nk(2n-3k-1)}
\sum_i\sum_{j\in\Nb^Z_k(i)\setminus\Nb^X_k(i)}\bigl(r^X_{ij}-k\bigr),
\end{equation}
with continuity obtained by exchanging the roles of $X$ and $Z$. The $k$-NN
overlap is the mean fraction of shared neighbours.

\paragraph{Density correspondence.}
With $\mathrm{dens}(x_i)=\bigl(k^{-1}\sum_{j\in\Nb_k(i)}d_{ij}\bigr)^{-1}$, the
log densities in the two spaces are compared by ordinary least squares slope,
residual, Spearman, and Pearson-on-logs. Note this is a different estimator from
the training-time quantity~\eqref{eq:logr} --- deliberately so, since a metric
that reuses the objective's own estimator cannot detect that the objective
optimised it at the expense of something else.

\paragraph{Uniformity.}
With $k$-NN radii $r_X(i),r_Z(i)$ on an interior mask ($k=10$ by default),
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:uniformity}
\mathrm{spacing\_cv}=\frac{\sd(r_Z)}{\overline{r_Z}},
\qquad
\mathrm{area\_sd}=\sd\!\left[\log\frac{r_Z}{r_X}\right].
\end{equation}
Zero $\mathrm{area\_sd}$ means every neighbourhood is magnified by the same
factor, i.e.\ the map is area-preserving up to global scale. The two are not
redundant and can disagree usefully: a layout can be perfectly uniform on the
page while badly misrepresenting a non-uniform sample, and
$\mathrm{area\_sd}$ is the honest target for that distinction.

\subsection{Matched nulls}
\label{sec:null}

Several of the metrics above take large values for trivial reasons on some
data --- $k$-NN overlap on iris is already $\approx0.86$ for a null map, and
geodesic Spearman on a uniformly sampled sheet is high for any monotone
projection. Every reported configuration is therefore refitted on
column-shuffled input, which destroys joint structure while retaining marginals,
and only the \emph{margin} over that null is treated as carrying information.
Two traps this exposes are worth naming, because both are easy to publish by
accident. On a uniformly sampled s-curve, PCA wins raw label-transfer accuracy
on the intrinsic parameter while scoring $\approx0.31$ on $k$-NN overlap: that
column rewards \emph{not} unrolling. And density Spearman is uninformative on
uniformly sampled feeds, where ambient density is constant by construction ---
the null makes this visible immediately.

\subsection{Persistence: would you get the same map twice?}
\label{sec:persistence}

Every statistic above measures whether \emph{a} map is good. None measures
whether refitting reproduces it --- which is the property a saved artefact
actually sells, since downstream coordinates, stored region labels, decision
thresholds, and published figures all assume the map is stable. We therefore
make it a protocol.

\begin{protocolx}[Persistence]
\label{prot:persistence}
Refit the same configuration at (i) $\ge3$ seeds and (ii) $\ge3$ training
subsamples ($80\%$ draws). For each unordered pair of resulting maps $(Z,Z')$
of the same points, report two quantities.
\end{protocolx}

\paragraph{Coordinate disagreement.}
The embedding is defined only up to a similarity, so the comparison fits a
similarity $(s,Q,t)$ by orthogonal Procrustes on a shared \emph{anchor} set and
scores the residual on \emph{held-out} points, normalised by the spread of the
reference map:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:procdis}
\mathrm{disagree}(Z,Z')
=
\frac{\med_{i\in\mathcal{E}}\ \norm{sQz_i+t-z'_i}}
     {\med_{i\in\mathcal{E}}\ \norm{z'_i-\bar z'}} .
\end{equation}
Fitting and scoring on the same points would understate disagreement, because
the alignment absorbs part of what is being measured.

\paragraph{Neighbourhood rank agreement.}
On a shared probe set, the mean Spearman correlation between the full neighbour
rank vectors the two maps induce, plus the Jaccard overlap of their top-$k$
neighbour sets, with self-distances excluded so a trivially shared zero cannot
inflate agreement.

\begin{remark}[Which number is primary, and why both are needed]
\label{rem:persprimary}
Rank agreement is primary and coordinate disagreement secondary. A similarity
transform can absorb a rotation but cannot repair genuine topological ambiguity:
which arm of a branching structure lands on which side is a discrete choice, and
no rigid motion undoes it. Large coordinate disagreement together with high rank
agreement is therefore a \emph{gauge artefact}, not instability, and should be
reported as such. The converse --- small coordinate disagreement with degraded
rank agreement --- is the genuinely bad case, and it is the one a
coordinates-only comparison would miss.
\end{remark}

The operational consequence for a practitioner is direct and appears in the
artefact contract: coordinates may be stored, thresholded, and cited only when
coordinate disagreement is small \emph{and} rank agreement is high; when only
rank agreement holds, the artefact supports neighbour queries and region
membership but not coordinate-valued claims.

\subsection{An independent reference geometry}
\label{sec:emd}

Every fidelity metric above is computed against the same ambient metric the map
was fitted under, which makes them a coherent but closed system. To probe
outside it we use, for image-valued data, the Earth Mover's Distance: exact
$W_1$ optimal transport of unit-normalised intensity across the pixel grid with
Euclidean ground cost between pixel centres, so that $W_1$ between a blob and
the same blob shifted by $s$ is exactly $s$. Pixel $\ell_2$ saturates at
$\sqrt{\norm{A}^2+\norm{B}^2}$ once two digits stop overlapping and says
nothing about how far apart they are; transport does not saturate and is linear
in deformation.

Because no embedder here is fitted on it, transport distance is an
\emph{independent} reference for scoring. It must be read as such: an
$\ell_2$-trained embedding scored against transport measures how much of an
alien geometry happens to leak into the map. It is a useful diagnostic and not
a verdict on fidelity, and we report it separately from the scorecard for that
reason (\S\ref{sec:emdresults}).

\subsection{Structured probes}
\label{sec:probes}

Held-out points test how a map handles \emph{more of the same}. They cannot
test what it does with something structured but genuinely new --- the case that
matters when a map triages unseen data. We therefore use a fixed family of
synthetic probes drawn on the same grid as the data and never shown during
training: twelve recognisable shapes (faces, cross, ring, bars, checker, disc,
block) plus two unstructured controls (noise, feature-permuted). Two properties
are deliberate. Every probe is rescaled to a target ink mass, by default the
median of the real data --- without this, a probe carrying twice a digit's ink
is detectable from total intensity alone and every out-of-distribution score is
inflated for a reason unrelated to geometry. And no ground-truth location is
claimed: these points are off-manifold by construction, so nothing asserts a
correct two-dimensional position for a smiley. They support only narrow claims:
that a probe is farther from the data than a held-out digit, that distinct
patterns stay distinct, and that they do not collapse.

Unless stated otherwise, every number is a mean over three seeds with the
sample standard deviation reported ($n=3$; five seeds on iris), at a $20\%$

This bar is not a formality: on digits, $5$-NN label accuracy alone carries a
seed standard deviation of $0.031$, which exceeds most of the differences one
would otherwise be tempted to interpret.

Three conclusions follow, and they are the most consequential results in the

\emph{Manifold maps are reproducible; the clustered map is not.} At fixed
training data, the s-curve and swiss roll return the same map to rank Spearman
$0.998$ and $3\%$ coordinate disagreement. Digits reaches only $0.712$, with a
Jaccard overlap of $0.377$ --- fewer than two neighbours in five are shared
between two runs differing \emph{only} in seed. Any downstream use of digits
coordinates as though they were stable is unsupported by our measurements. This
is not a defect specific to \code{leanmap}; it is a property of fitting a
two-dimensional layout to data whose structure is a set of well-separated
clusters, where the relative arrangement of clusters is underdetermined. The
contribution here is that the instability is \emph{measured and reported}
rather than assumed away.

\emph{The s-curve subsample row is the gauge artefact of
Remark~\ref{rem:persprimary} in pure form.} Coordinate disagreement rises
eightfold ($0.032\to0.264$) while rank agreement is essentially unchanged
($0.998\to0.995$). Reading the coordinate number alone would call this a large
instability; it is a reparameterisation.

\emph{Subsampling is the harder perturbation, and not uniformly so.} The swiss
roll degrades genuinely under subsampling (rank $0.998\to0.862$) rather than
merely moving, which is a real sensitivity of the unrolling to which points are
present --- and a warning that a map fitted to a pilot sample may not survive
the full campaign.

$0.043$ of $\mathrm{area\_sd}$, both resolved --- the ramp is not merely
unnecessary there but harmful, presumably because the anchor already carries
global structure and the coarse levels then compete with the fine ones for the
edge budget. The coarse-heavy default is retained for the general case at
$\lambda_{\mathrm{geo}}=0.15$, and \S\ref{sec:defaults} now pairs raising
$\lambda_{\mathrm{geo}}$ on smooth manifolds with flattening the weights.

\paragraph{Other ladders, briefly.}
$\lambda_{\mathrm{anchor}}=0$ is unresolved from $1$ on any swiss-roll mean when
the frame term is active --- consistent with local rigidity already carrying
metric fidelity --- but is markedly less \emph{stable}: $\mathrm{area\_sd}$ is
$0.131\pm0.072$ with the anchor off against $0.075\pm0.008$ with it on, roughly
an order of magnitude more seed spread. The default stays at $1$. The
multiplicity exponent $\beta\in\{0,0.5,1\}$ is inert on digits (every metric
differs by less than $0.01$, none resolved), which is the expected and useful
negative: digits has no duplicate mass for the reweighting to act on, which
confirms $\beta$ is a property of the corpus rather than a solver knob. With the
PCA skip enabled, a $10$--$20\times$ head and hypernetwork rate recovers the
$0.098$ of geodesic Spearman the skip otherwise costs, down to an unresolved
$0.009$, while trustworthiness and $k$-NN overlap remain resolved-worse than the
no-skip control in every arm --- so the multiplier improves the skip path
without making it preferable.

\code{leanmap} is $231\times$ faster than \code{UMAP.transform} per point at

comparison is not read as \code{leanmap} being fast in absolute terms.

\begin{remark}[What is actually eliminated]
\label{rem:notindexfree}
The neighbour search is not removed. It is \emph{replaced}: an exact
variable-size search against the training set becomes a fixed $L\times D$ dense
distance computation against the landmarks, followed by a forward pass. That is
a different computation with a different answer, not a faster route to the same
one; the speed comes from having decided in advance how many comparisons
inference is allowed to make. The trade is nevertheless the right one for the
intended use, and for structural rather than constant-factor reasons: no index
to build or keep warm, no training data shipped with the model, and a
differentiable map that composes into a downstream network. The last has no
analogue in the interpolating column at any speed.
\end{remark}

The artefact size difference is asymptotic rather than the $944$ against $605$
KB visible at this $N$. \code{UMAP.transform} retains the training data and its
neighbour graph, so the serialised model grows linearly --- measured at $431$
bytes per point, flat to within $1\%$ over $N=250$ to $1797$. The
\code{leanmap} artefact is $L\times D$ landmarks plus weights and is independent
of $N$. The crossover is near $N\approx2200$; by $N=10^6$ the interpolating
artefact is roughly $400$ MB against an unchanged $944$ KB. In the opposite
regime the argument reverses and should be stated as such: at $N=150$ (iris) the
pickled training set is $25$ KB against a $664$ KB encoder, and only the
throughput advantage ($\approx26\times$ at $B=64$) and the existence of a
transform at all survive.

\section{Guidance: producing an artefact worth keeping}
\label{sec:guidance}
%==============================================================================

The remainder of the paper is addressed to the practitioner who intends the map
to outlive the figure. Everything here is a consequence of the measurements
above; nothing in it is specific to \code{leanmap} except the names of the
quantities.

\subsection{Operating procedure}
\label{sec:procedure}

\begin{protocolx}[Fit, freeze, gate, revisit]
\label{prot:operating}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
\item \textbf{Explore.} Fit at several configurations on a representative
  sample. Read the diagnostics of Table~\ref{tab:diagnostics} before reading the
  picture.
\item \textbf{Choose the metric before the hyperparameters.} The ambient metric
  is a scientific statement about which measurements are similar; it is not a
  tuning knob and should not be selected by downstream score.
\item \textbf{Establish persistence.} Run Protocol~\ref{prot:persistence}. Decide,
  \emph{before} using the map, whether it supports coordinate-valued claims or
  only neighbour-valued ones.
\item \textbf{Freeze.} Save the artefact. Freeze $\varepsilon$, the landmarks,
  the natural scale, and the calibration scores with it. Record the
  configuration and the diagnostic table.
\item \textbf{Gate, then look.} Score new batches with the ambient support
  score and gate on the conformal $p$-value; plot only after gating. Reversing
  the order invites reading a position that the artefact does not certify.
\item \textbf{Monitor.} Track the batch-level Mann--Whitney test against the
  calibration scores and the FiLM clamp hit rate. Both are cheap and both move
  before fidelity does.
\item \textbf{Revisit deliberately.} Refit when the batch test rejects
  persistently or when a new region is intended to enter the frame. Refitting is
  a versioning event: publish the new artefact alongside the old, with the
  rank agreement between them reported.
\end{enumerate}
\end{protocolx}

The step most often skipped is (5). A parametric map will return coordinates
for any input, and a plot made before gating is a plot in which anomalies are
indistinguishable from data by construction.

\subsection{Coupled knobs}
\label{sec:defaults}

Three pairs of settings are single decisions and are the most common source of
confusing results when treated as independent.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item \code{pca\_skip}, $\mathrm{lr}$, and \code{pca\_lr\_mult}. With the skip
  on at a low rate, the unconstrained linear path supplies most of the layout
  and pins digits label accuracy near PCA's. With the skip off at the same rate,
  the near-zero head is undertrained and scores worse still. Escape is either
  skip-off with $\mathrm{lr}\approx2\times10^{-2}$ (the shipped small-$N$
  recipe) or skip-on with a $10$--$20\times$ multiplier on the head and
  hypernetworks. A compromise rate is not an option.
\item $\lambda_{\mathrm{geo}}$ and \code{pyramid\_level\_weights}. With a strong
  anchor on a smooth manifold, flat weights are resolved-better than the
  coarse-heavy ramp (\S\ref{sec:ablations}). Raise one and flatten the other.
\item $\varepsilon$ and $\beta$. Together they decide how much density a
  duplicate-heavy region is credited with. Changing either without stating the
  other leaves the density claim ambiguous.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{A reporting card}
\label{sec:card}

We suggest that a published map be accompanied by the following, which is
short enough to be a caption or a table and sufficient for a reader to decide
what the map supports.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
\item The ambient metric and its natural scale.
\item $N$, $D$, $d$, $R$, $L$, $k$, the number of pyramid levels actually
  built, and $\hat m$.
\item $\varepsilon$, its estimation scope, and $\beta$.
\item Every non-default loss weight and ramp.
\item Persistence: rank Spearman, Jaccard@$k$, and coordinate disagreement,
  over seeds \emph{and} subsamples.
\item Fidelity metrics with their matched nulls, and seed spread.
\item The support score in use, $n_{\mathrm{cal}}$, the resolution floor
  $1/(n_{\mathrm{cal}}+1)$, and the operating $\alpha$.
\item $\nu$, the median affinity perplexity, the triplet retention and its
  measured null, and the $k$-NN recall.
\item An explicit statement of whether coordinates or only neighbour rankings
  are claimed to be stable.
\end{enumerate}

Item 9 is the one that most changes how a map is used downstream, and it is
answerable only by running Protocol~\ref{prot:persistence}.

\subsection{Worked settings by data type}
\label{sec:recipes}

\begin{description}[leftmargin=0pt,style=nextline]
\item[\textnormal{Smooth sheets and folded manifolds}
  (trajectories, reaction coordinates, deformation series).]
  Raise $\lambda_{\mathrm{geo}}$ to $0.5$ and flatten the level weights. Enable
  the frame term with $\code{frame\_tangent}=\text{True}$ and a delayed ramp
  $(0.5,0.75)$ if the manifold folds back. Prefer geodesic or Poisson-disk
  landmarks. Expect high persistence and treat coordinates as usable. Watch
  $\nu$: a manifold with a hole will show it.

\item[\textnormal{Clustered corpora}
  (compositions, phases, discrete structural families).]
  Keep $\lambda_{\mathrm{geo}}$ low; leave the frame term off unless local
  metric fidelity matters more than separation. Expect low coordinate
  persistence: publish rank-based claims and region memberships, not positions.
  The density term will fight the neighbour terms here, because the density
  signal \emph{is} the cluster structure; the tail gate is what makes it
  tolerable, and setting $\lambda_{\mathrm{dens}}=0$ is a legitimate choice.

\item[\textnormal{Duplicate-heavy databases}
  (re-depositions, redundant entries, stable-state streams).]
  This is what the $\varepsilon$-net and the halo pass exist for. Set $\beta$
  from the meaning of a repeat, not from a sweep. Report the compression ratio;
  it is the single number telling a reader how much of the corpus was distinct.

\item[\textnormal{Distribution-valued features}
  (pair-distance distributions, spectra, histograms).]
  Use a distributional metric --- $\ell_1$ between normalised curves, or a
  transport distance --- via the metric layer, and consider a scale--shape view
  factorisation (\S\ref{sec:viewmetric}) so that magnitude becomes a monotone
  axis rather than a folded-in direction. Check the differentiability flag: with
  a non-differentiable metric the landmarks freeze after initialisation, which
  is correct but means landmark placement quality is entirely decided by the
  selection scheme.

\item[\textnormal{Streaming or campaign data.}]
  Fit on a pilot, then \emph{do not refit per batch}. The persistence results
  show that refitting is not a neutral operation. Gate incoming batches; when
  the batch test rejects persistently, that is evidence about the experiment,
  not only about the map.
\end{description}

%==============================================================================
\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}
%==============================================================================

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{The support model is coarse.} The domain of validity is a union
  of $L$ ellipsoidal charts, which is a low-resolution description of any real
  manifold. The repair certificate is loose in exactly this way: repaired points
  pass by construction while remaining roughly twice as far from real data under
  an independent transport metric as a genuine held-out point. Tightening
  $\alpha$ cannot fix a shape mismatch.
\item \textbf{The conformal test is about cover, not identity.} A shift that
  leaves the support score unchanged --- sliding along the manifold --- is
  invisible, and a novel point lying on the manifold will pass. The test answers
  ``is this near the region I chart'', not ``have I seen this''.
\item \textbf{Coordinate persistence on clustered data is poor.} Two runs
  differing only in seed share fewer than two digits neighbours in five. We
  believe this is a property of the problem, not of the method, but we have not
  demonstrated that, and we do not claim coordinate stability there.
\item \textbf{The conditioning architecture is under-justified by our own
  measurements.} FiLM is resolved against its concatenation control on one
  metric of one dataset (\S\ref{sec:filmvsconcat}).
\item \textbf{Scale.} Every measurement reported here is at $N\le2\times10^3$.
  The construction is designed for $N$ up to $10^6$ --- inverted-file and
  approximate-neighbour paths, subsample-corrected $\varepsilon$, a pyramid that
  only becomes four levels deep above $N\approx1.7\times10^4$ --- and those
  paths are exercised by tests but not by the results here.
\item \textbf{Charts assume a vector space.} The tangent-chart score is linear
  in the primary view; for a non-linear view the isotropic ball fallback is
  correct but gives up the high-dimensional discrimination of
  \eqref{eq:chartscore}.
\item \textbf{Mondrian categories are synthetic.} The taxonomy is generated from
  the data --- matched noise and feature permutation --- and therefore covers
  the failure families those constructions represent, not the ones a particular
  instrument actually produces. A domain-specific taxonomy would be stronger and
  the machinery accepts one.
\item \textbf{The comparison is under an imposed metric.} We compare methods
  under the same ambient geometry, which is the fair head-to-head, and we report
  a cross-metric transfer result separately in which \code{leanmap} does worse.
  We have not run the experiment in which both methods are fitted from a
  transport-derived graph.
\end{enumerate}

%==============================================================================
\section{Relation to prior work}
\label{sec:related}
%==============================================================================

\paragraph{Fuzzy neighbour embedding.}
The geometric loss and the $(a,b)$ kernel follow
UMAP~\cite{mcinnes2018umap,spivak2012fuzzy}, as does the $\rho/\sigma$
smoothing and the fuzzy union. The departures are the parametric map, direct
differentiation instead of a clipped-force approximation, the multi-scale
Galerkin pyramid, and the metric regularisers. Parametric variants of neighbour
embedding have been studied for $t$-SNE~\cite{vandermaaten2009parametric} and
UMAP~\cite{sainburg2021parametric}; the FiLM-conditioned encoder here is in
that spirit, with landmark affinities as the conditioning
code~\cite{perez2018film} and an optional PCA residual path.

\paragraph{Global geometry.}
The landmark geodesic backbone and Procrustes anchor import the Isomap
gauge~\cite{isomap2000} and classical scaling~\cite{torgerson1952} as a soft
prior rather than as the embedding itself, and report the non-embeddability
that the standard eigenvalue clamp discards. Landmark-based scaling has a long
history~\cite{desilva2004landmark}; the contribution here is that the same
landmarks serve as index, code, and support model.

\paragraph{Local structure.}
In tangent mode the frame term is closest to local tangent space
alignment~\cite{zhang2004ltsa}: both estimate a weighted local tangent basis and
require the tangent-coordinate structure to agree between data and embedding.
It differs in matching Gram matrices with a detached scale rather than aligning
coordinates through a global eigenproblem, and in acting as a penalty on a
parametric map. It reads equally as as-rigid-as-possible
deformation~\cite{sorkine2007arap} expressed through neighbour Grams. The
density term follows densMAP~\cite{narayan2021densmap} in shape, and departs
from it in constraining only the ordering, for the reason given in
\S\ref{sec:density}.

\paragraph{Multi-scale graphs.}
Galerkin coarsening is standard in algebraic multigrid and has been used for
graph layout and coarsened spectral methods; the specific choices here --- edge
contraction rather than node-distance recomputation, a strictly monotone
$q_{0.99}$-anchored squash, and component-restricted bridging --- are motivated
by the ablations of \S\ref{sec:ablations}.

\paragraph{Conformal prediction.}
Cover and chart scores yield finite-sample distribution-free control under
exchangeability~\cite{vovk2005,shafer2008tutorial}; Mondrian
(category-conditional) calibration follows~\cite{vovk2003mondrian}, and the
amortised distance-to-support head uses conformalised quantile
regression~\cite{romano2019cqr}. The combination we are not aware of elsewhere
is the packaging: a support model, its calibration, and a closed-form repair
onto its acceptance region shipped \emph{inside} the embedding artefact, so
that the domain of validity travels with the map.

%==============================================================================
\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
%==============================================================================

We have described a neighbour embedding designed to be an instrument rather
than an illustration: a parametric map that can be saved and called, with a
declared domain of validity carried inside the same artefact, and with the
evidence needed to audit it recorded alongside. The mathematics is set out in
full because reuse requires it --- an artefact whose merge radius, temperature
calibration, or gauge target cannot be inspected is not reusable, only
redistributable.

Two results seem to us more general than the method. The first is that
\emph{persistence must be measured}: the property a saved map actually sells is
that refitting reproduces it, no standard fidelity metric measures that, and
when we measured it the answer separated sharply by data type --- manifolds
reproduced to rank correlation $0.998$, a clustered corpus to $0.712$ with
under two neighbours in five shared. Any field that publishes embedding
coordinates as findings has an untested assumption here. The second is that
gating and viewing are different operations with different tools: an ambient
support score detected every structured probe we constructed, while map-space
distance caught under half of them, and the map-space number is the one a reader
would naturally compute. Both results are, at bottom, about the difference
between what a map shows and what it certifies.

The most useful extensions are in the support model. The union of ellipsoidal
charts is the loosest component of the current design, and the repair
certificate quantifies exactly how loose. A conditional-coverage construction
that survives the calibration-set sparsity of Remark~\ref{rem:mondrianbucket},
and a taxonomy of anomaly families drawn from instrument physics rather than
from synthetic perturbation, would both tighten what the artefact is able to
promise.

\section{Order of operations at fit time}
\label{app:order}
%==============================================================================

The order below is fixed, and several steps are ordered for validity rather
than convenience.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
\item Split a conformal calibration set from \emph{raw} $X$, before any
  deduplication, landmark selection, or normalisation. This is what makes the
  training-fitted radii and charts of \S\ref{sec:cover} disjoint from
  calibration by construction (Remark~\ref{rem:validity}).
\item Fit encoder normalisation $(\mu,\sigma)$ on the encoder view of the
  training split.
\item Resolve the metric specification and fit the natural scale
  $s_{\mathrm{nat}}$ and, for composite metrics, the per-block scales $c_f$.
\item Initialise per-factor anchors on their views; the primary factor's
  anchors are the graph landmarks.
\item Build the graph pyramid: estimate $\varepsilon$, grow the
  $\varepsilon$-net, run the halo pass, select landmarks, search neighbours,
  smooth memberships, symmetrise, reweight by multiplicity, add the backbone,
  and coarsen.
\item Freeze the resolved $\varepsilon$ into the saved configuration.
\item Measure the density budget from the ambient graph, before any
  optimisation.
\item Instantiate the factor stack and the encoder; seed the PCA path if
  enabled.
\item Fit $(a,b)$ from \code{min\_dist}.
\item Build the geodesic pack: landmark geodesics, classical MDS, and the
  negative-eigenvalue diagnostic.
\item Optionally warm start: rank candidate layouts, extend the winner by the
  Nystr\"om interpolation, and regress the encoder onto it.
\item Optimise~\eqref{eq:total} under the level budget and ramp schedule.
\item Fit the support model on \emph{training} points.
\item Calibrate on the held-out split; store sorted scores and the parameter
  hash.
\item Serialise: weights, landmarks, temperatures, metric name and scale,
  resolved configuration, calibration scores, and the diagnostic record.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Cost model}
\label{app:cost}
%==============================================================================

Let $E_0$ be the finest-level edge count, $B_e$ the per-step edge budget, $S$
the number of steps, $W$ the backbone width and $K$ its depth.

\paragraph{Fit.}
Graph construction is dominated by the neighbour search: $\mathcal{O}(N^2D)$ in
brute mode, and $\mathcal{O}(N c \bar n_b D)$ in inverted-file mode with
$\bar n_b$ the mean bucket size, plus one Dijkstra per landmark
($\mathcal{O}(L\,(E\log N))$) when a geodesic construction is requested. The
$\varepsilon$ estimation is $\mathcal{O}(N^2D)$ exactly, or $\mathcal{O}(N\log
N)$ with an index. Coarsening is linear in the edge count of the level being
contracted. Training is $\mathcal{O}\bigl(S\,B_e\,(LD + KW^2)\bigr)$ for the
geometric term, with the frame and density terms adding
$\mathcal{O}(S\,C\,m\,(LD+KW^2))$ for $C$ stars of width $m$, and the geodesic
term adding one forward pass over all $L$ landmarks per step.

\paragraph{Inference.}
One evaluation costs $L$ distance computations ($\mathcal{O}(LD)$), one
hypernetwork pass ($\mathcal{O}(L\cdot 128 + 128\cdot 2KW)$), and one backbone
pass ($\mathcal{O}(DW+KW^2)$). All are independent of $N$, which is requirement
R2. The support score reuses the same $L$ distances, so gating is free relative
to embedding; the chart score adds $\mathcal{O}(LDm)$.

\paragraph{Artefact size.}
$LD$ landmarks, $L$ temperatures, $DW+KW^2+Wd$ backbone and head parameters,
$2KW\cdot 128 + 128L$ hypernetwork parameters per factor, $L(1+Dm+2)$ support
parameters, and $n_{\mathrm{cal}}$ calibration scores. Independent of $N$
throughout.
